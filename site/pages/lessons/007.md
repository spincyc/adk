# Potentiometer input

Status: **Host verified; physical acceptance pending**<br>
Energy class: **E1** — low-voltage Mega analog input and one current-limited LED

This lesson makes the evidence chain visible:

`shaft position → TP-A0 voltage → raw sample → scaled value → LED duty`

The potentiometer provides a stable, repeatable signal before the course adds
ambient-light variation. Measure TP-A0 relative to GND, predict the corresponding
ADC range, then compare the prediction with the LED output. TP-A0 proves the
electrical input; it does not by itself prove that sampling, calibration, or
PWM output succeeded.

Use a 10 kΩ linear potentiometer between the Mega 5 V and GND rails, with its
wiper on A0/TP-A0. Put a 220 Ω or larger resistor in series with the LED. With
USB power removed, place the meter clips at TP-A0 and GND and inspect the circuit;
only then apply power. Measure the board's 5 V rail—the ADC result is a ratio
to that reference, not proof of an exact 5.000 V supply.
Record the exact USB source or computer port, its stated current capability or
configured current limit, and the USB cable identity; use no unrecorded hub or
alternate supply.

`AnalogInput` owns one analog-capable pin. Sampling remains explicit, and raw
ADC counts stay available beside the directly scaled brightness in optional
115200-baud records. D13 gives a 250 ms acquisition pulse followed by a 750 ms
separator; D6 PWM and the resistor-limited LED remain the primary non-Serial
observation. The sketch shuts every owner down after a fixed 120-second run.

Stop and remove USB power if TP-A0 is outside the board rails, a part becomes
warm, the USB connection resets, or the LED disagrees with the commanded safe
state. Verify high impedance only with the PDF's one-pin-at-a-time 10 kΩ/10 kΩ
midpoint fixture, installed while USB is removed. Hardware acceptance remains
open until exact specimen identities, voltage endpoints, resistor and LED
current, startup, rollback, reset, shutdown, and power-removal observations
are recorded.
