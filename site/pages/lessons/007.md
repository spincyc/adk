# Potentiometer input

Status: **host verified; hardware experimental**<br>
Energy class: **E1** — low-voltage Mega analog input and one current-limited LED

This lesson makes the evidence chain visible:

`shaft position → TP1 voltage → raw sample → calibrated value → LED duty`

The potentiometer provides a stable, repeatable signal before the course adds
ambient-light variation. Measure TP1 relative to GND, predict the corresponding
ADC range, then compare the prediction with the LED output. TP1 proves the
electrical input; it does not by itself prove that sampling, calibration, or
PWM output succeeded.

Use a 10 kΩ linear potentiometer between the Mega 5 V and GND rails, with its
wiper on A0/TP1. Put a 220 Ω or larger resistor in series with the LED. With
USB power removed, place the meter clips at TP1 and GND and inspect the circuit;
only then apply power. Measure the board's 5 V rail—the ADC result is a ratio
to that reference, not proof of an exact 5.000 V supply.

`AnalogInput` owns one analog-capable pin. Sampling remains explicit, and raw
ADC counts stay available beside the directly scaled brightness. The circuit
uses PWM brightness as its primary observation path; Serial output is optional
supporting evidence.

Stop and remove USB power if TP1 is outside the board rails, a part becomes
warm, the USB connection resets, or the LED disagrees with the commanded safe
state. Hardware acceptance remains open until the voltage endpoints, resistor,
LED current, startup, and shutdown observations are recorded.
