# The tabletop rover light show

Prerequisites: **Lessons 001–020**

Build a rover you can see without connecting a motor. Six USB-powered LEDs
show left and right forward, reverse, and enable intent while the real
`RoverController`, `UltrasonicRanger`, and two `MotorIntent` objects run a
fixed story:

`observe synthetic range and wheels → decide the route → actuate six lamps`

This is model-only synthesis of the Lessons 019–020 decision interfaces. It
does not carry forward the ultrasonic circuit or motor-driver hardware.
Direction lamps show direction, and enable lamps show only whether applied
duty is nonzero; unlike Lesson 020's PWM lamp, they do not show duty magnitude.

The story starts automatically, drives forward, turns, freezes for a
one-second pretend obstacle, resumes, and finishes dark. A fixed deadline
shuts every output down at 12 seconds.

## Complete supported circuit

Use an Arduino Mega 2560, six ordinary LEDs, six 1 kΩ resistors, a breadboard,
and jumpers. Each LED cathode returns to the breadboard negative rail, and
that rail returns to a labeled Mega GND pin.

| Lamp | Mega pin | Breadboard path |
|---|---:|---|
| Left forward | D22 | 5a; resistor 5c–5f; LED 5h–6h |
| Left reverse | D23 | 10a; resistor 10c–10f; LED 10h–11h |
| Left enable | D24 | 15a; resistor 15c–15f; LED 15h–16h |
| Right forward | D25 | 20a; resistor 20c–20f; LED 20h–21h |
| Right reverse | D26 | 25a; resistor 25c–25f; LED 25h–26h |
| Right enable | D27 | 30a; resistor 30c–30f; LED 30h–31h |

For every row, the LED long leg is the anode beside its resistor. Jumper the
short-leg row to the negative rail. Unplug USB before changing a wire.

The printable lesson contains a literal Mega-header and breadboard-coordinate
drawing. Do not connect a motor, driver, relay, battery, wheel, or external
supply.

## Three quick wins

1. Build D22–D24 first. The three left lamps already show one imaginary wheel.
2. Add D25–D27. Both banks drive together, then disagree during a turn.
3. Watch near 2.5 seconds. Both enable lamps go dark while synthetic range is
   150 mm; they resume after the fixed range returns to 500 mm.

The built-in D13 LED turns on only after every software object initializes.
At route completion the six story lamps go dark. At 12 seconds D13 also turns
off as the sketch releases every output.

## What is real

The route state machine, obstacle policy, reversal dead time, snapshots, and
six applied-intent signals are real software behavior. The range echo and
wheel edges are deterministic simulations. No claim is made about ultrasonic
accuracy, encoder capture, motor current, torque, stopping distance, or
physical motion.

Automated repository tests cover the detailed route, fault, timing, replay,
restart, and shutdown cases behind the scenes. They are not extra learner
chores.

## Why the project stops at LEDs

A powered rover needs exact driver and motor identities, primary electrical
documentation, measured stall current, a separate current-limited supply,
flyback and thermal design, a rated physical disconnect, guarding, and real
bench evidence. A retail kit-family listing establishes none of those facts.
The USB-only light show is therefore the complete Lesson 021 circuit.
