# Deterministic bench rover

Status: **host verified; E1 indicator experiment and E2 physical acceptance open**
Supported experiment: **E1** — USB-powered Mega 2560 and seven LEDs
Deferred continuation: **E2** — motors only after a specific power design passes review

Lesson 021 composes `UltrasonicRanger`, two `MotorIntent` objects, and
`RoverController`. The canonical sketch runs a deterministic simulated range
and wheel plant, so the same high-level loop can be explored without a motor,
H-bridge, encoder, or external supply:

`observe simulated range and wheels → decide rover motion → actuate LED evidence`

`RoverController` owns a bounded copy of a static route. Each `RoverInput`
supplies a `RangeReading`, its observation time, two
`RoverWheelObservation` records, start and stop events, and the current
`TimePoint`. Its snapshot preserves that evidence alongside left and right
`MotorCommand` values, mode, stop reason, route progress, deadlines, status,
and transition count. It owns no pin, motor, or clock.

## Indicator circuit

Each external LED has its own 1 kΩ resistor. Connect its cathode to the named
Mega GND. Do not connect a motor driver or motor supply.

| Evidence | Mega pin | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Left forward | D22 | left applied direction is forward |
| Left reverse | D23 | left applied direction is reverse |
| Left enable | D24 | left applied duty is nonzero |
| Right forward | D25 | right applied direction is forward |
| Right reverse | D26 | right applied direction is reverse |
| Right enable | D27 | right applied duty is nonzero |
| Acquisition | D13 | every first-class object initialized |

The six motor witnesses present applied intent, not motor current or motion.
D13 proves the acquisition sequence completed; it does not prove that the six
outputs are safe. To check the inactive state separately, predict all six
external LEDs dark, observe them after shutdown, then measure D22–D27 against
the named GND according to the endpoint’s high-impedance procedure.

## Exact composition

`UltrasonicRanger` converts an explicit echo trace into a `RangeReading`. The
sketch synthesizes that trace: it reports 500 mm normally and 150 mm from
three through four seconds. No ultrasonic module is connected in this E1
experiment.

`RoverController` applies the route and safety policy. It emits stopped
commands on an operator stop, invalid or stale range, invalid wheel evidence,
missing initial motion, excessive wheel mismatch, route timeout, unexpected
motion while stopped, or invalid time. Stop dominates a simultaneous start.
An obstacle at or below 200 mm enters `ObstacleHold`; motion may resume only
at or above 250 mm.

The two `MotorIntent` objects then bound each command to duty 160 and enforce
100 ms of inactive dead time before a direction reversal. Their explicit
`ExternalPowerDomainGate` is a command-admission boundary in this simulation;
it is not a physical power disconnect.

The route is:

1. drive forward for eight simulated wheel edges;
2. turn left for four edges;
3. pause for 500 ms;
4. drive forward for eight edges;
5. finish with both commands stopped.

## Predict, observe, interpret

Before reset, predict the complete LED sequence. Then observe:

1. D13 turns on only after all seven LEDs, both `MotorIntent` objects, the
   ranger, and the rover controller initialize.
2. Both forward and both enable witnesses turn on for the first route step.
3. A left turn requests left reverse and right forward. The affected
   `MotorIntent` first presents a dark dead-time interval.
4. The simulated 150 mm obstacle makes both enable witnesses dark.
5. At route completion, and on any failure, all six witnesses are dark.

Interpret the result narrowly. The sequence supports deterministic
composition and the presented logic intent. It does not prove ultrasonic
accuracy, encoder capture, motor polarity, torque, stopping distance, or
removal of load power. Serial is optional and is not used by the sketch.

## Host evidence

The deterministic tests cover lifecycle and invalid configuration, every
route action, exact obstacle hysteresis boundaries, stop dominance and
explicit restart, invalid and stale range, encoder faults, motion-start and
wheel-mismatch timeouts, route timeout, unexpected motion, rollover, bounded
route storage, and identical replay.

The software stage is supported by host tests and the Mega compile gate. The
E1 circuit still needs its recorded bench card; no physical observation is
claimed.

## Deferred E2 continuation

Do not infer a generic H-bridge circuit from this lesson. A future motor stage
must name the exact driver and motor revisions, cite their primary
documentation, record stall current and every supply/thermal limit, and show
separate logic and motor-power paths. It also requires a rated physical
load-power disconnect, current limiting, restrained wheels raised clear of
the bench, and observed startup, reset, sensor loss, stall, timeout, reversal,
shutdown, and loss-of-control behavior.

The software stop is not an emergency stop. Lesson 021 carries no person,
animal, hot item, sharp tool, or hazardous material. Floor operation remains
outside the supported experiment.
