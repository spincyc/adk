# Lesson 070 — descriptor-driven threshold modules

Status: **Host verified E0 deterministic component; exact no-LTO resource
review complete, exact powered specimens and bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied declarations and copied frames only**
Safety boundary: **no module, ADC channel, digital pin, pull resistor, supply
rail, power switch, timer, interrupt, bus, display, stimulus, clock, storage
medium, or powered fixture**

Lesson 070 gives analog/comparator modules one explicit vocabulary without
pretending that every similar-looking PCB is the same device:

`declared fixture descriptor + attributable copied frame → structural validity + explicit comparator meaning`

`ModuleThresholdDescriptor` declares what one fixture contract means.
`ModuleThresholdFrame` carries one copied pair of analog/comparator channels
with complete provenance. Stateless functions validate both values and derive
comparator assertion only when the descriptor states the polarity.

This is a software contract, not a universal sensor driver, module catalog, or
permission to apply power. Structural validity proves that a declaration is
internally coherent. It does not prove that the declaration is true of a
physical specimen.

## One descriptor instead of one class per PCB

Many low-voltage modules present an analog output, a comparator output, or
both. Their outward resemblance hides consequential differences: active
polarity, output stage, pull requirement, rail, usable signal range, warm-up,
settling, and the direction of a threshold control may all vary.

The descriptor keeps those facts separate:

| Field group | Declared meaning |
|---|---|
| identity | schema, descriptor, specimen, and electrical-evidence revisions |
| channels | analog only, comparator only, or analog plus comparator |
| comparator output | unspecified, push-pull, open-drain, or open-collector |
| pull | unspecified, none, pull-up, or pull-down, with an explicit rail |
| electrical ranges | declared supply and signal millivolt intervals |
| analog domain | declared minimum and maximum raw endpoint codes |
| comparator polarity | unspecified, active-high, or active-low |
| threshold control | unspecified, fixed, or potentiometer |
| control direction | unspecified, increasing clockwise, or increasing counterclockwise |
| timing | independently declared warm-up and settling durations |

Every identity and revision is nonzero. Ranges are ordered and inclusive.
These values are declarations used to validate copied evidence; they are not
measured ratings and cannot select pins, resistance, ADC reference, supply, or
an endpoint.

Marketplace aliases, PCB color, connector count, bundle position, and
`KY-`-style numbers are documentation clues, not electrical identity. Two
descriptors may share behavior while retaining distinct specimen and
electrical-evidence revisions.

## Unspecified means unknown, not “probably”

`Unspecified` is a supported E0 declaration for output stage, pull,
comparator polarity, threshold-control kind, or direction. It is not an
invalid enum and never selects a convenient default.

A structurally valid descriptor with required unspecified facts has incomplete
declarations. `moduleDescriptorDeclarationsComplete()` exposes that
structural distinction. A true result means only that every required
declaration is present and internally coherent. It is not physical authority,
E1 admission, specimen qualification, or proof that any declaration is true.

Cross-fields are canonical:

- analog-only descriptors leave comparator fields unspecified;
- comparator-bearing descriptors declare a coherent output stage and
  polarity before assertion can be derived;
- pull rail is unspecified when no pull is declared;
- a declared pull requires a declared rail;
- fixed or absent threshold controls do not invent a potentiometer direction;
  and
- unused fields retain their one canonical representation.

Invalid underlying enum encodings always reject. A valid `Unspecified` value
is therefore different from corrupted or unsupported input.

## Known zero is different from unknown time

Warm-up and settling each pair a declaration with a `Duration`.
`ModuleDurationDeclaration::Known` with a zero duration means explicitly that
no interval is required. `Unknown` means that the interval is not established
and requires a canonical numeric zero.

Lesson 070 reads no clock, delays nothing, and never decides that a physical
source is warm or settled. A duration declared `Unknown` can never have its
corresponding satisfaction flag asserted. The frame's
`declaredWarmupSatisfied` and `declaredSettlingSatisfied` fields are copied
declarations bound to its provenance. Lesson 071 will apply supplied time and
characterization-run ordering.

## Attributable copied frames

`ModuleThresholdFrame` repeats the descriptor schema, identity, revision,
declared specimen reference and revision, and declared electrical-evidence
revision. It then adds nonzero source identity, source-configuration revision,
sequence, and supplied observation time. A frame therefore cannot drift to a
different declared specimen or evidence basis while retaining the same short
descriptor identity.

Each channel has an explicit `ModuleChannelStatus`:

- `NotPresent`
- `Current`
- `Stale`
- `ProducerFault`

The analog channel carries a raw endpoint code and its complete producer
status. The comparator channel carries physical-level-shaped copied data,
presence, status, producer status, and the asserted semantic value derived
from the descriptor.

Topology controls the canonical absent representation. A comparator-only
frame cannot smuggle in an analog value. An analog-only frame cannot publish
a comparator level or assertion. Presence, channel status, producer status,
value, and descriptor topology must agree.

A rail endpoint remains a raw endpoint code. Lesson 070 does not diagnose it
as open, short, stuck, saturated, dark, bright, hot, cold, wet, loud, or any
other physical condition. Those conclusions require a specific experiment
and belong to Lesson 071 or a separately qualified endpoint.

## Comparator assertion is explicit

The same copied logic level has opposite meaning under active-high and
active-low descriptors:

| Declared polarity | Copied level | Derived assertion |
|---|---:|---:|
| `ActiveHigh` | low | false |
| `ActiveHigh` | high | true |
| `ActiveLow` | low | true |
| `ActiveLow` | high | false |

`moduleComparatorAsserted()` returns a `Result<bool>`. Unspecified or invalid
polarity returns failure; the helper never guesses.

Polarity says nothing about whether the output is push-pull, open-drain, or
open-collector. Output stage says nothing about which rail or resistance is
safe. An onboard comparator LED may also invert, load, or merely parallel an
output; its visible state cannot define the software contract.

## Public API

Lesson 070 is stateless. It owns no policy object, lifecycle, callback,
buffer, clock, resource, or codec:

```cpp
Status validateModuleThresholdDescriptor (
    const ModuleThresholdDescriptor& descriptor) noexcept;

Status validateModuleThresholdFrame (
    const ModuleThresholdDescriptor& descriptor,
    const ModuleThresholdFrame& frame) noexcept;

Result<bool> moduleComparatorAsserted (
    const ModuleThresholdDescriptor& descriptor,
    bool comparatorLevelHigh) noexcept;

Result<bool> moduleDescriptorDeclarationsComplete (
    const ModuleThresholdDescriptor& descriptor) noexcept;
```

Malformed configuration returns `InvalidConfiguration` or `InvalidArgument`.
Descriptor/frame identity mismatch returns `InvalidArgument`. An attributable
channel producer failure is different: it remains a structurally valid copied
frame with `ModuleChannelStatus::ProducerFault`.

Validation never repairs a descriptor, substitutes a value, or publishes a
replacement frame. There is no Lesson 070 record image. Lesson 072 owns the
future stable characterization-record format; a checksum or digest cannot
substitute for fieldwise descriptor facts or physical identity.

## Synthetic desk experiment

The E0 experiment uses copied fixtures only. Do not connect a module or upload
the compile-only replay to powered hardware.

Prepare four fixtures:

1. a complete active-high analog/comparator declaration;
2. the corresponding active-low declaration;
3. an open-collector declaration with an explicit pull-up rail; and
4. a structurally invalid declaration or a valid E0 declaration whose
   required electrical fields remain unspecified.

For each fixture, predict descriptor validity, declaration completeness,
frame validity, comparator assertion, and the fields that must be canonically
absent. Then validate copied frames at raw-domain endpoints, with not-present
channels, and with independent producer faults. Reorder and replay the same
fixtures; fieldwise outcomes must remain identical.

| Fixture | Predict | Observe | Interpret |
|---|---|---|---|
| active-high, level high | descriptor and frame valid; asserted true | topology, polarity, assertion, and status cells | polarity was declared rather than inferred from a module name |
| active-low, level high | descriptor and frame valid; asserted false | the same level beside the opposite assertion cell | logic level and semantic assertion remain distinct |
| open-collector plus pull-up | structurally valid with complete declarations when every required field is present | output-stage, pull, rail, and completeness cells | one field did not silently imply the others, and completeness did not claim physical truth |
| comparator-only frame | analog channel is canonically absent | analog status/raw and comparator cells | topology prevented a phantom analog observation |
| producer fault | frame remains attributable | channel status, producer status, identity, and sequence cells | a source fault did not become malformed input |
| unspecified polarity | descriptor may be valid at E0; assertion fails and declarations are incomplete | validation, assertion-result, and completeness cells | unknown meaning did not become a guessed default |
| unknown warm-up marked satisfied | frame rejects | timing declaration, copied flag, returned status, and the complete unchanged input | unknown time could not masquerade as elapsed time |
| malformed cross-fields | validation rejects | returned status and a fieldwise comparison of the complete unchanged input | structural failure did not repair or mutate caller evidence |
| byte-identical replay | every fieldwise result repeats | two fixture-result ledgers | order and host timing did not change the outcome |

The compile-only Mega sketch exposes named volatile cells for descriptor
identity and revision, topology, polarity, pull and rail, raw value,
comparator assertion, channel status, declared warm-up/settling satisfaction,
declaration completeness, and returned status. These cells are the E0 non-Serial
observation path. Optional Serial output may mirror them but cannot replace
them.

Resource-acquisition evidence is exactly zero endpoint and registry calls.
Safe-state evidence is separately the absence of any hardware intent. Neither
result proves a physical module is unpowered, a pin is high impedance, or a
rail is safe.

## Deterministic verification

The passing host matrix covers every valid and invalid enum encoding; every
topology, output, pull, rail, polarity, control, and direction cross-product;
valid E0 unspecified fields and explicit declaration incompleteness; identity and
revision boundaries; equal and inverted ranges; raw endpoints; known-zero,
known-nonzero, and canonical unknown durations; every channel topology and
status; active-high/active-low assertion; producer-fault collisions;
descriptor/frame mismatch; fieldwise replay; and proof of zero fake endpoint,
clock, transport, registry, and power calls.

Every validation input is passed as const data. Tests retain a complete
fieldwise copy and prove that accepted and rejected calls leave every input
field unchanged. They compare defined fields, not raw C++ padding, and do not
invent an output object or persistence claim.

The canonical Mega 2560 replay measures 4,564 bytes flash and 684 bytes static
SRAM. The exact no-LTO probe measures:

| Metric | Measured | Target | Hard limit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| flash | 3,570 B | 10 KiB | 14 KiB |
| static SRAM | 362 B | 768 B | 1,024 B |
| synchronous stack | 74 B | 320 B | 448 B |
| descriptor value | 45 B | 64 B | 96 B |
| frame value | 38 B | — | — |
| complete Lesson 070 object composition | 83 B | 192 B | 256 B |
| residual SRAM after static, stack, and 128 B reserve | 7,628 B | 4,096 B | 3,072 B floor |

Every Lesson 070 target and hard gate passes. The fingerprint binds the exact
sketch, source, public header, probe, boundary configuration, compiler, core,
and flags. These are compiler/linker and storage measurements, not electrical
or physical-module evidence.

## What remains open

E1a begins with one exact low-voltage specimen, not a family nickname. It
requires both-face photographs and markings, exact pinout, primary device
documentation plus an authoritative module schematic, or that documentation
plus a reviewed trace of the populated module when no schematic exists.

Only then may a separate powered acceptance establish supply and signal
voltages, quiescent and peak current, source impedance, output stage, pull
rail and resistance, inactive behavior, threshold-control direction,
warm-up, settling, and analog/comparator correlation. It needs named AO, DO,
VCC, and GND test points, current-limited first energization, explicit power
removal, and separate resource-acquisition and safe-state observations.

The module's onboard comparator LED is supporting evidence only. A future E1
fixture needs an independently current-limited visible validity/fault
indicator, while Lesson 072 may compose a qualified display beside the raw
and comparator evidence. No wiring table, pin assignment, or formal schematic
is authorized by this E0 lesson.

Ascending and descending sweeps, threshold estimates, hysteresis, chatter,
stuck output, rail interpretation, and analog/digital disagreement belong to
Lesson 071. Presentation and one volatile characterization record belong to
Lesson 072. Durable storage is outside this arc.

Register devices, unknown emitters, flame or combustion, flammable/toxic/
unknown gas exposure, physiological or medical claims, body contact, food
safety, generic analog-temperature boards, capacitive-touch generalization,
immersion, mains, high voltage, and unattended stimulus remain excluded.
