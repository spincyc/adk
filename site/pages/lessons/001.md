# Digital output

Status: **host verified; Mega hardware acceptance pending**

`DigitalOutput` is the first debugging primitive. It owns one pin, writes the
initial latch before enabling output, and returns the pin to high impedance
before releasing its claim.

Energy class: **E1** for the Mega 2560 factory LED and current-limited USB
power; the deterministic host exercise is E0. Physical high-impedance
acceptance additionally requires a measured weak-bias resistor and meter.

```cpp
adk::Runtime       runtime;
adk::DigitalOutput led (runtime.resources (), LED_BUILTIN);
```

The example makes its evidence finite and repeatable:

1. successful acquisition produces one 250 ms pulse;
2. a 750 ms dark interval separates acquisition from behavior;
3. exactly three 500 ms high/500 ms low behavior cycles run;
4. `shutdown()` releases the pin, and the sketch makes no more hardware calls.

Reset repeats the complete sequence. USB removal at the computer end is the
physical stop and power-removal method. Software shutdown and a dark LED do not
by themselves prove high impedance. The host fake trace must end in `INPUT`;
physical proof separately uses an identified, measured 10 kΩ weak pull-up from
D13 to the measured 5 V rail, installed with USB removed. Record the exact
board's D13 LED circuit and calculate the expected driven-LOW and released-pin
voltage ranges before power. If either is unresolved, leave the physical stage
blank. Physical Mega acceptance remains pending.

Measured physical acceptance remains in the controlled campaign rather than
as learner paperwork in this lesson.

Stop for heat, smoke, odor, unexpected power flicker, repeated USB disconnects,
damage, or a loose connector. Remove USB before touching or reconfiguring the
board.
