# Lesson 067 — normalized inertial records

Status: **Host verified E0 deterministic component; exact powered adapters
and bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied source-frame values and caller-owned record images
only**
Safety boundary: **no sensor, GPIO pin, interrupt, I2C bus, supply rail,
clock, display, storage medium, moving object, or powered fixture**

`InertialRecordNormalizer` turns one complete copied Lesson 043
`InertialSample` and an `InertialRecordConfig` into one attributed
source-frame `InertialRecord`. `InertialRecordCodec` gives that value one
canonical 64-byte image:

`copied six-axis sample + source provenance + revisions → normalized record + fixed image`

The component does not acquire, calibrate, rotate, qualify, compare, or store
a sample. It preserves values that a caller already expressed in micro-g and
milli-degrees per second. Its output is a deterministic representation of
copied evidence, not proof of a physical MPU6050, QMI8658, motion event,
orientation, or durable recording.

## Why the record starts before qualification

Lesson 043 already defines the source-frame units and provenance needed to
interpret a copied six-axis sample. Its `InertialObservationPolicy` also
classifies freshness and deliberately rejects the still-unqualified physical
adapter kinds. Lesson 067 does not reach through that policy because doing so
would discard or retain evidence before a record could preserve it.

Instead, Lesson 067 consumes the complete copied `InertialSample` directly.
It records not-ready and producer-fault events as attributable outcomes rather
than substituting the most recent successful vector. Lesson 068 will qualify
one explicitly configured record source and apply an explicit axis mapping.
Lesson 069 will bind qualified records to one recorder session. Neither later
lesson may reinterpret a checksum or model label as physical identity.

## One complete source-frame value

The normalized record preserves:

- nonzero record-schema and normalization-contract revisions;
- source kind, model, nonzero source identity, configuration revision, and
  calibration revision;
- declared acceleration and angular-rate ranges;
- all three acceleration axes in micro-g;
- all three angular-rate axes in milli-degrees per second;
- supplied observation time and nonzero producer sequence;
- data-ready and saturation evidence;
- the complete producer `Status`; and
- one derived record state.

`InertialRecordState` is closed:

| State | Input evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `Recorded` | producer `Ok` and data ready | one copied six-axis sample is present |
| `NotReady` | producer `Ok` and not ready | the source reported no new payload |
| `SourceFault` | producer status is not `Ok` | the copied producer fault remains attributable |

State is derived from the copied readiness and producer status. The caller
cannot assert a conflicting state. Both `NotReady` and `SourceFault`
canonicalize readiness false, saturation `None`, and all six vector values to
zero so an old or untrusted payload cannot masquerade as a current sample.
Source identity, revisions, ranges, time, sequence, and producer status remain
attributable.

## Normalized does not mean transformed

The word “normalized” describes the fixed record contract and units. It does
not mean a unit-length vector, orientation solution, gravity removal, bias
subtraction, sensor fusion, or axis remapping.

Acceleration remains a signed 32-bit source-frame vector in micro-g. Angular
rate remains a signed 32-bit source-frame vector in milli-degrees per second.
Values are preserved exactly, including signed extrema. The component performs
no negation or magnitude calculation, so it does not invoke signed-overflow
repair on `INT32_MIN`.

Each axis must remain inside its declared inclusive range. A value exactly at
the positive or negative boundary is valid. One normalized unit beyond the
boundary is contradictory even if the producer reports saturation.
Saturation is evidence of bounded measurement loss, not permission to clip or
admit an out-of-range value.

The component preserves source, model, range, and revision labels without
claiming that they authenticate hardware. Copied MPU6050- and QMI8658-labeled
fixtures can exercise the record format at E0; they do not establish that an
adapter exists or that a delivered part matches the label.

## A canonical 64-byte image

The cross-build identity is a fixed 64-byte image, not the memory layout of a
C++ object:

| Bytes | Field |
|---:|---|
| 0–3 | magic |
| 4 | image-format version |
| 5 | image length |
| 6–9 | schema and normalization-contract revisions |
| 10–17 | record state, source identity, configuration, and calibration |
| 18–19 | reserved zero |
| 20–27 | acceleration and angular-rate ranges |
| 28–51 | acceleration and angular-rate vectors |
| 52–59 | observation time and producer sequence |
| 60–61 | readiness/saturation flags and producer status |
| 62–63 | CRC-16 over bytes 0–61 |

Every multi-byte field uses little-endian order. Reserved bits and bytes are
zero. Encoding begins with a zero-filled candidate, writes every field,
computes the checksum, and copies the complete image to caller storage only
after validation succeeds. Decoding likewise stages a complete candidate
record and publishes it only after the entire image validates.

A decoded image must encode back to the same 64 bytes. Unknown revisions or
enum values, nonzero reserved fields, alternative flag spellings, and
noncanonical state combinations are not aliases. C++ padding, native
endianness, `bool` representation, and `memcmp` over public objects are never
part of the format.

The CRC detects accidental corruption. It is not authentication, provenance,
hostile-input protection, media durability, or proof that a sensor produced
the bytes. Full fields remain authoritative; a checksum or future digest
cannot substitute for fieldwise comparison.

## Atomic failures

Malformed input returns a `Status` and leaves the caller’s complete output
byte-for-byte unchanged. This includes invalid enum domains or source/model
pairs, zero required identity or revision fields, invalid ranges, impossible
state combinations, noncanonical images, and checksum failure. Tests place
canaries around both value and image outputs so partial publication is
observable.

A well-formed non-`Ok` producer status is different from a codec failure. It
encodes successfully as `SourceFault`, preserving its attribution while
canonicalizing the untrusted payload fields. Likewise, a well-formed
not-ready event encodes as `NotReady`; its vectors are explicitly zero rather
than an implicit replay of the last good vector.

Lesson 067 preserves observation time and sequence but does not compare them.
It does not infer age, freshness, gaps, duplicates, regressions, rollover, or
session membership. Those policies belong to Lessons 068 and 069.

## Deterministic replay

The canonical Mega 2560 sketch is compile-only. It uses copied fixtures and
one caller-owned image, then exposes named volatile result cells as the
non-Serial observation path.

| Stage | Predict | Observe | Interpret |
|---:|---|---|---|
| ready sample | `Recorded` and one canonical image | state, identity, six axes, and image bytes | copied evidence was represented without acquiring a source |
| not ready | `NotReady` | false readiness, zero vectors, `None` saturation, identity, and sequence | no stale payload was retained as a new sample |
| producer fault | `SourceFault` | complete status, zero vectors, and attribution cells | a source fault remained evidence rather than a codec failure |
| range boundary | accepted without clipping | signed axis and saturation cells | inclusive range semantics are explicit |
| round trip | the decoded value matches every field | equality and checksum cells | the fixed image, not object padding, is cross-build identity |
| corrupted byte | decode rejects atomically | status, output canaries, and prior result | no partial record escaped validation |

Before each call, predict the state, fields, image outcome, and unchanged
output on rejection. Optional Serial text may summarize the same evidence,
but it is not the only proof.

Resource-acquisition evidence at E0 is exactly zero pins, timers, interrupts,
buses, registry entries, sensors, and power resources. Because the component
has no physical output or resource, its separate safe-state evidence is the
absence of any hardware intent. Neither observation proves that a powered
sensor is idle or disconnected.

## Verification and resource scope

The passing host matrix covers every enum and source/model combination,
every producer status, all readiness and saturation states, exact signed and
range boundaries, zero and maximum valid revisions and sequences,
source/model labels used only as copied attribution, encode/decode golden
images, every-byte corruption, wrong byte order, nonzero reserved fields,
checksum failure, output canaries, and two independent fieldwise replays.

The canonical Mega 2560 replay measures 5,852 bytes flash and 632 bytes static
SRAM. The normalizer owns only its immutable two-revision configuration; the
codec has no mutable object state, and its image is exactly 64 bytes.

These tests and measurements establish deterministic E0 representation only.
They cannot prove electrical behavior, sensor identity, conversion scale,
calibration,
mounting, data-ready timing, accuracy, noise, drift, physical safe state, or
record persistence.

## What remains open

An exact MPU6050 or QMI8658 adapter requires its own architecture and powered
acceptance campaign. That work must establish exact chip and carrier
revision, primary register documentation, supply and logic levels, regulator
and level-shifter population, pull-ups and address straps, identity and reset
behavior, range configuration, byte order, scale conversion, data-ready and
saturation semantics, axis convention, cadence, and named electrical
observation points.

Evidence for one family cannot qualify the other. A retail kit label, copied
model enum, shared identity value, or record checksum is insufficient.

Axis mapping, stationary bias evidence, source qualification, voting,
fallback, failover, multiple-record buffering, clock ownership, display
control, media access, acknowledgement, torn-write recovery, and durability
are also outside Lesson 067. Adding any of them reopens the architecture
boundary rather than quietly extending the record codec.
