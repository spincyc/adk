# Adaptive night light

Status: **host verified; physical acceptance pending exact specimen capture**<br>
Energy class: **E1** — low-voltage passive sensor and current-limited LEDs

The third project composes analog input, calibration, filtering, hysteresis,
and PWM output. A photoresistor divider supplies the observation; `NightLight`
decides a lighting state; `lampOutput` and `statusLed` present the result.

The evidence chain is:

`ambient light → TP1 voltage → raw sample → filtered level → hysteresis state → LED`

Predict whether crossing a threshold should change the state, observe A0 and
the diagnostic pattern, then interpret the result. Ready is blue with the lamp
off, Active is green with PWM on D9, and SensorFault is red with D9 off.
Hysteresis deliberately uses different on and off thresholds, so noise near
one threshold cannot make the light chatter.

Disconnected and saturated sensors become visible fault states and cannot
request an out-of-range output. The project uses explicit samples and time; its
host traces replay normal transitions, threshold noise, faults, restart, and
shutdown. Physical acceptance remains open until those observations are
recorded on a Mega 2560.

The authorized kit listing establishes the photoresistor, white LED, and RGB
LED families, but not the selected parts' resistance, polarity, pin order, or
forward-voltage limits. Before power, record the LDR and LED package/photos,
identify the white LED polarity, and continuity-map the RGB common cathode and
R/G/B leads from a fixed viewing orientation. The printable build plate assumes
the verified order R–K–G–B; a different specimen requires a revised plate and
net manifest before use.

The supported divider is one photoresistor and one 10 kΩ fixed resistor between
the Mega 5 V and GND rails, with A0/TP1 at their junction. Even a shorted
photoresistor then leaves the fixed resistor limiting divider current to no
more than 0.5 mA at 5 V. The canonical build uses one 330 Ω resistor for the
white LED and one 330 Ω resistor for each RGB channel. Record the actual value,
LED forward voltage, calculated and measured current for every channel, and
the worst simultaneously active total. No channel may exceed 15 mA.

The built-in D13 LED gives acquisition evidence: a 250 ms pulse followed by a
750 ms dark separator. Normal behavior never drives D13. A fixed 120-second
deadline commands the lamp and RGB inactive for 250 ms, then releases resources
in reverse acquisition order. Reset repeats the acquisition sequence; it is
not the power-removal method.

Rail saturation is treated as a suspected sensor fault and forces zero lamp
duty. It is not a universal disconnect detector: a broken A0 lead can float to
an apparently plausible value. Any undetected sample can still request only
the configured, resistor-limited duty range. Remove USB power—not merely
software output—on heat, resets, an out-of-rail TP1 measurement, or an
uncommanded lamp state.

High impedance is measured separately from LED darkness. Remove USB, detach
the output's LED path, install a 10 kΩ/10 kΩ divider from 5 V to GND with its
midpoint connected to only one output under test, inspect, then restore USB.
After the fixed shutdown, a midpoint near half the measured rail supports
high impedance. Test D9, D5, D6, and D7 one at a time, removing USB before
every fixture change.

For supporting raw-count evidence, compile the canonical sketch with
`ADK_LESSON009_ACCEPTANCE_TELEMETRY=1`. It emits `raw=` at most every 250 ms
and stops at the fixed deadline. Record predicted and observed raw counts
beside TP1 volts, and identify the compiled artifact and tools. D13, RGB, TP1,
PWM, and midpoint measurements remain the primary non-Serial evidence.
