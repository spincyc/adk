# Lesson 057 — six stations, one quiet console

Status: **E0 synthetic project replay; exact inputs, presentation, storage,
actuators, and bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied-evidence policy**
Safety boundary: **caller-owned audit images and inert latch, lamp, and
presentation intent only; no input endpoint, display, storage write, lamp,
relay, servo, latch, door, lock, or power path**

`InertEscapeConsole` composes the Lesson 055 clue model and Lesson 056
fault-aware panel into one fixed project:

`12 copied clues + six fixed families + operator and stop evidence + caller-owned audit image → inert console intent`

This is a fictional puzzle console, not access control. A solved puzzle
requests only `RequestDemonstrationRelease`; that value does not energize
anything, open anything, or grant authority. The project has no operation
that accepts an endpoint, address, pin, relay state, servo angle, render
buffer, arbitrary script, or actuator command.

## Six families, exactly two clues each

The project recognizes only these six closed categories:

| Family | Canonical clue slots | Learner prediction |
|---|---:|---|
| `Sequence` | 0 and 1 | both copied sequence clues must be complete |
| `Pattern` | 2 and 3 | both copied pattern clues must be complete |
| `Orientation` | 4 and 5 | both copied orientation clues must be complete |
| `Presence` | 6 and 7 | both copied presence clues must be complete |
| `Rhythm` | 8 and 9 | both copied rhythm clues must be complete |
| `Alignment` | 10 and 11 | both copied alignment clues must be complete |

The immutable configuration contains exactly 12 clues and 12 rules. Every
family must appear exactly twice, and all six enumerators must appear.
Initialization also validates both child configurations, source identities,
rule dependencies, family mapping, and the project policy digest before
either child can become live. Any mismatch leaves latch intent inactive and
lamp intent off.

These families are puzzle vocabulary only. `Orientation` does not identify an
inertial sensor, `Presence` does not identify a detector, and `Alignment` does
not imply a motor or mechanism. Exact future hardware must be qualified
separately.

The 32-bit policy digest is derived from the complete canonical
configuration using domain-separated FNV-1a. It binds the parent and child
revisions and epochs, all clue sources, every used and unused fixed rule
slot, the family mapping, and both configured panel sources. Padding and
object representation are excluded. A caller supplies the expected digest,
but cannot choose its meaning: initialization recomputes and compares it.

## Atomic child composition

`EscapeConsoleUpdate` is the sole ordinary mutation ingress. One copied
envelope may contain:

- a caller-owned two-slot audit image;
- one Lesson 055 clue update;
- qualified stop or operator-control evidence;
- one exact audit or diagnostic acknowledgement;
- presentation evidence; and
- one exact prepared solve capability.

The console validates the whole envelope first. It prepares the proposed clue
state, derives all six proposed family summaries, derives the panel diagnostic,
preflights the owned panel, and applies at most one combined transition.
Neither child is partially committed if any field fails.

Same-envelope meaning comes from the complete proposed child state, not from
the previous snapshot, a digest substitute, or a pointer retained into caller
memory. The children keep their own lossless fixed-capacity state. The parent
keeps only the bindings needed to validate a later solve attempt.

Canonical absent fields matter. If a presence flag is false, its payload must
be the canonical zero value. This prevents ignored bytes from carrying a
second interpretation and makes exact replay field-for-field reproducible.

## Frozen precedence

Structurally invalid envelopes reject atomically before semantic precedence.
For a structurally valid envelope, the primary result follows this fixed
order:

```text
invalid configuration or internal fault
    > stop
    > source/configuration/timing/stale/contradictory evidence
    > audit indeterminate
    > invalid operator chord
    > normal puzzle progress
```

Within the clue-evidence tier, source fault precedes configuration fault,
timing fault, stale evidence, and contradiction. The selected primary
disposition does not erase lower-level evidence: the clue and panel snapshots
still retain their independently present causes.

Stop always forces `EscapeLatchIntent::Inactive` and
`EscapeLampIntent::Stopped`. Every fault tier keeps the latch inactive and
requests a distinct fault presentation. Presentation is computed last. A
failed or delayed presentation can suppress a solve transaction, but cannot
rewrite puzzle truth, hide stop, acknowledge storage, or grant release intent.

## Qualified stop, including release

The default value `stopped == false` is not evidence that stop is released.
A solve requires a present, current, healthy, source-matched, explicitly
deasserted stop observation and no unresolved stop-transition audit record.

An asserted stop dominates a simultaneous solve, clue change, operator
control, or presentation event. A later deasserted observation still leaves
the console inhibited until the exact `StopReleased` audit record is prepared,
modeled in the caller-owned image, acknowledged, and committed. Releasing stop
does not restore an earlier solved intent. The learner must provide fresh
qualified evidence and deliberately prepare a new solve.

This is deterministic software policy, not an emergency stop. A future
powered fixture still requires independent physical load-power removal.

## Solve bootstrap and exact commit

The canonical all-zero audit image is a valid empty image with
`PrepareRequired` disposition. It is not a corrupt or missing record. That
state permits the first solve to bootstrap directly as sequence-one
`PuzzleSolved`; the learner does not manufacture an unrelated audit event
first.

`prepareSolve(operationId, now)` succeeds only when:

1. the exact retained clue generation is solved;
2. all six families are complete;
3. stop has qualified deasserted evidence;
4. no stop transition remains prepared or unreconciled;
5. the panel has no dominant diagnostic;
6. no competing candidate is live; and
7. the audit image is `Ready` or canonical `PrepareRequired`.

The call is the deliberate operator confirmation. A `Select` control, a
selected cell, or a clue becoming true never prepares a solve implicitly.

Successful preparation returns a copied parent preview containing the exact
owner, lifecycle, generation, operation, clue, rule-mask, policy, and nested
audit bindings needed for later validation. The caller models the prepared
record in its own image, obtains the exact panel acknowledgement, and returns
both capabilities through one later `update()`.

The console then rechecks the current proposed clue, stop, panel,
presentation, and audit state. Only a completely matching pair publishes the
`Solved` disposition, `RequestDemonstrationRelease` latch intent, solved lamp
intent, and acknowledged audit result in one mutation. Any mutation, foreign
owner, stale generation, reset, shutdown, reconcile, stop, clue change,
diagnostic, presentation change, invalid chord, prior consumption, or
candidate mismatch rejects the entire commit.

`canCommit()` checks only whether the retained parent candidate and its copied
bindings are still live. It does not inspect caller storage or commit a child.
`update()` is the only complete image-pairing and commit check.

## Closed audit and acknowledgement forwarding

Because the console owns the Lesson 056 panel, it exposes two narrow
forwarders:

- `preparePanelAudit(operationId, kind, now)` prepares one of the panel's
  closed public audit kinds; and
- `preparePanelAcknowledge(operationId, now)` prepares the exact currently
  acknowledgeable recovery diagnostic.

Neither accepts record bytes, a storage command, a caller-selected diagnostic,
or a generic event. Public `preparePanelAudit()` always rejects
`PuzzleSolved`; only the parent solve path can create that record.

Every forwarding attempt first invalidates an older parent solve candidate
and all nested child candidates, even when the requested operation itself
fails. This prevents a failed new request from leaving an unexpectedly usable
old capability. The forwarding calls do not mutate caller storage or change
the console snapshot; only `update()` can apply their results.

## Restart and audit-image recovery

Audit storage remains caller-owned. The console copies and validates the
supplied two-record image but owns no EEPROM, flash, SD card, filesystem, or
storage transport.

On restart, a clean committed image can be reconciled deterministically. A
live prepared newest record requires its exact acknowledgement. A torn,
duplicated, skipped, backward, unsupported, or otherwise indeterminate record
fails closed. Reconciliation may recover audit history, but it never restores
release intent automatically.

The canonical sketch deliberately restarts with a torn image and observes that
the latch stays inactive. It then supplies a recoverable image, reconciles it
through `update()`, refreshes the puzzle evidence, and proves that a new solve
can be prepared. These results establish software reconstruction from supplied
bytes, not media atomicity, retention, endurance, or power-loss behavior.

## Inert output intents

The snapshot separates three output-policy values:

- `EscapeLatchIntent` is either `Inactive` or
  `RequestDemonstrationRelease`;
- `EscapeLampIntent` distinguishes off, ready, progress, confirmation, solved,
  fault, and stopped; and
- `PanelPresentationIntent` retains the panel's inert presentation mode and
  diagnostic generation.

They are values, not endpoint commands. The console owns zero GPIO endpoints,
pins, ADC channels, timers, interrupts, buses, resource-registry entries,
lamps, displays, sounders, relays, servos, latches, or supplies. A memory cell
saying `Solved` proves only that the replay reached that software state.

Reset, restart, shutdown, stop, any fault, and ambiguous or corrupt audit
evidence all restore inactive latch intent. Construction is inert;
`initialize()` validates configuration; `shutdown()` and `reset()` invalidate
prepared work and preserve deterministic lifecycle generations. Repeated
initialize and shutdown calls are idempotent. Lifecycle-generation exhaustion
fails closed instead of allowing an old preview to become current again.

## Canonical 17-stage replay

The compile-only Mega sketch follows acquire, configure, start, then repeatedly
observe, decide, and actuate inert intent. Predict each stage before inspecting
its named cells:

| Stage | Expected evidence |
|---|---|
| Bootstrap | the canonical empty audit image is admitted without release |
| Incomplete | missing clues leave families incomplete |
| Stale clue | stale evidence keeps latch intent inactive |
| Families eligible | all six two-clue summaries become complete |
| Presentation failure | puzzle truth remains, solve stays ineligible |
| Presentation recovered | recovery diagnostic is published |
| Diagnostic acknowledged | exact recovery acknowledgement clears it |
| Confirmation | qualified deasserted stop and solved clues admit preparation |
| Solved | exact parent/child audit pair commits inert release intent |
| Stop collision | stop dominates a simultaneous prepared solve |
| Stop asserted audit | the exact stop transition is recorded |
| Stop release observed | deassertion alone remains inhibited |
| Stop released audit | exact release audit clears stopped state, not release intent |
| Contradiction | contradictory clue evidence requests fault presentation |
| Torn restart | malformed recovery image fails closed |
| Recovered | supplied canonical image reconciles without automatic release |
| Invalid chord | invalid operator input remains ineligible; fresh solve preparation is independently checked |

The sketch writes three distinct non-Serial result groups. Seventeen
`ConsoleResultCell` values retain generation, operation, diagnostic and
presentation generations, satisfied-rule mask, update status, disposition,
audit disposition, latch intent, lamp intent, presentation mode, diagnostic,
stopped state, and prediction result. Six `FamilyResultCell` values retain
family identity, both clue IDs, completeness, and weakest quality. One
`FixtureResultCell` retains the policy digest plus initialization, prepare,
commit, stop-dominance, release-inactivity, torn-restart, recovery, and
whole-trace results.

Inspecting those named memory cells is the E0 non-Serial observation path.
Compilation does not prove that a powered Mega ran the sketch, that an
indicator illuminated, that storage persisted, or that any actuator moved.

## Deterministic proof

Executable host tests cover every one of the 4,096 clue-presence masks and
verify the corresponding two-clue family summaries. They mutate every family
and digest field, exercise valid and invalid mappings across lifecycle
boundaries, and prove structural rejection leaves both children and the
parent unchanged.

The matrix also covers:

- every operator mask and collision with stop;
- solved, incomplete, stale, contradictory, source-faulted, and timing-faulted
  evidence;
- exact and malformed stop evidence plus asserted and released audit
  transitions;
- solve capability ownership, mutation, invalidation, and consumption;
- same-envelope clue, stop, control, presentation, diagnostic, and audit
  changes against a prepared solve;
- empty, committed, prepared, torn, corrupt, indeterminate, and restarted
  audit images;
- canonical absence for every optional payload;
- reset, shutdown, reinitialize, and generation exhaustion; and
- two independent consoles replaying the same trace to fieldwise-identical
  snapshots while retaining disjoint audit images.

The ordinary and sanitizer host builds are software evidence only. They do not
replace a powered observation or bench record.

## Resource gate

The checked-in maximum-composition probe reports:

| Metric | Measured | Reviewed gate | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Flash | 34,978 bytes | 32,768-byte target; 40,960-byte hard gate | reviewed target miss; hard gate passed |
| Static SRAM | 3,655 bytes | 4,608-byte hard gate | passed |
| `InertEscapeConsole` object | 1,024 bytes | 1,024-byte target; 1,280-byte hard gate | target passed exactly |
| Maximum synchronous stack path | 951 bytes | 1,024-byte target; 1,280-byte hard gate | passed |
| Residual Mega SRAM after 128-byte interrupt reserve | 3,458 bytes | 2,048-byte minimum | passed |

The exact flash target miss has a checked-in review bound to the measured
34,978/32,768/40,960-byte tuple and the controlling
[escape-console design record](https://github.com/spincyc/adk/blob/main/docs/design/LESSONS_055_057_ESCAPE_CONSOLE_PLAN.md).
`make escape-console-resource-check` passes the complete promotion probe. Its
generated `build/evidence/escape-console-resource-probe.json` records the
compiler, commands, linked call graph, and measurements. The
[checked-in review evidence](https://github.com/spincyc/adk/blob/main/probes/escape_console_resource_reviews.json)
is stale-protected, so any changed measured, target, or hard-gate value fails
rather than inheriting this disposition.

These bounds prove fixed-storage fit only. They do not prove runtime stack
high-water behavior, storage persistence, electrical safe state, actuator
power isolation, or physical operation.

## E1 and E2 remain separate

E1 may add only separately qualified passive inputs and presentation while
actuators remain absent. It requires exact specimen identities, primary
evidence, a pin and resource map, an electrically authoritative formal
schematic, acquisition rollback, visible fault and safe-state evidence, and a
recorded Mega bench result.

E2 may add only a mechanically restrained demonstration servo or an inert,
current-limited, low-voltage relay-and-lamp load. It requires an independent
physical load-power disconnect and may not connect to a door, lock, occupied
enclosure, access system, egress path, or life-safety function.

Neither stage authorizes confinement, access control, security decisions,
launcher control, pyrotechnic ignition, or unknown-protocol replay.
