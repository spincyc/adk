# Lesson 056 — fault-aware operator panel

Status: **E0 deterministic policy replay; exact passive-input, presentation,
and storage fixtures remain open**

Energy class: **E0 copied values and semantic intent only**
Safety boundary: **no input endpoint, display, storage medium, actuator, latch,
lock, door, or power path**

`FaultAwareOperatorPanel` combines copied operator evidence, copied
presentation evidence, and a complete caller-owned two-slot audit image:

`one atomic input envelope → navigation, diagnostic, audit, and presentation intent`

The panel is a puzzle-teaching policy object. It is not access control,
authentication, authorization, an emergency stop, a protective interlock,
confinement, egress, or life-safety equipment.

## One copied envelope

Construction copies a fixed configuration: configuration revision, instance
epoch, maximum input age, selectable-cell count, and distinct control and stop
source identities. The panel retains no caller pointer. Each `update()` call
receives one complete `FaultAwareOperatorPanelInput` value containing explicit
time and presence-qualified control, stop, presentation, diagnostic,
acknowledgement, and audit-image evidence.

Structural validation happens before mutation. A noncanonical absent field,
unknown enum, wrong source identity, changed same-sequence payload, future or
ambiguous timestamp, malformed preview, or malformed audit image rejects the
whole envelope and preserves the prior snapshot and canonical image.

Control evidence uses bits 0 through 3 for Previous, Next, Select, and
Acknowledge. Zero bits means no command, one bit is a single control, and two
or more bits publish `InvalidChord`; the panel never selects a winner from a
chord. Bits 4 through 7 are invalid evidence and reject atomically.

## Stop dominates, but it is not an emergency stop

After structural validation, a qualified asserted stop dominates navigation,
acknowledgement, presentation feedback, audit progress, and puzzle-related
values in the same envelope. It invalidates live preview capabilities,
preserves the selected cell, publishes `Stopped`, and requests stopped
presentation.

Stop is retained level-sensitive policy evidence. Restart can restore it only
from the newest canonical committed `StopAsserted` audit record. Clearing it
requires a newer qualified deasserted observation from the configured stop
source and commitment of that exact `StopReleased` record. Reset, a display
success, a control chord, or an unrelated acknowledgement cannot clear it.

This software state does not remove electrical power. A later physical fixture
must have an independent means to remove load power; no person or animal may
depend on this panel to enter, leave, or remain safe.

## Diagnostics and presentation provenance

The snapshot separates invocation status from domain state.
`Status` reports whether the envelope was structurally admitted.
`PanelDiagnostic`, `OperatorChordDisposition`, and
`PanelAuditDisposition` report the admitted policy result.

Only two diagnostics are acknowledgeable:

| Diagnostic | Exact origin |
|---|---|
| `InputRecovered` | a newer qualified control or stop observation clears the immediately preceding attributable input fault |
| `PresentationRecovered` | successful presentation evidence for the exact current intent follows the immediately preceding attributable presentation failure |

Both are generated internally and bound to the current diagnostic generation.
Injecting either through the external diagnostic field rejects. Configuration,
internal, source, timing, stale, contradictory, invalid-chord, stopped,
indeterminate-audit, and corrupt-audit conditions cannot be acknowledged.

`PanelPresentationEvidence` binds success or failure to one exact
`intentGeneration`. Presentation failure may request a fault presentation and
later produce `PresentationRecovered`, but it never changes stop authority,
commits an audit record, or turns puzzle policy into success. Rendering remains
the caller's responsibility.

## Prepare, write, acknowledge

The audit image is a copied latest-two-record value, not an append-only log and
not proof of physical persistence. Empty slots are all zero. Every nonempty
record is self-describing, canonical, and protected by fieldwise 32-bit
FNV-1a digests; padding and raw C++ object bytes are never hashed.

The publication sequence is explicit:

1. `prepareAudit(operationId, kind, now)` creates and retains one exact
   `Prepared` candidate without changing the caller's image.
2. The caller models a storage write by copying `preview.record` into
   `image.slots[preview.slotIndex]`.
3. A later atomic `update()` supplies that complete observed image, sets
   `auditAcknowledgePresent`, and returns the exact `PanelAuditPreview`.
4. If every image, record, predecessor, owner, lifecycle, generation,
   operation, checksum, and digest field matches, the panel publishes the
   record as `Committed` and consumes the capability.
5. The caller obtains the complete canonical value with
   `canonicalAuditImage()`.

There is one bootstrap case: an all-zero image may receive a canonical
sequence-one prepared record without a predecessor. Later records require the
adjacent committed predecessor. The two slots are then reused by replacing
only the unambiguously older committed record.

| Complete image form | Disposition |
|---|---|
| two empty slots | `PrepareRequired` |
| one committed record and one empty slot | `Ready` |
| two adjacent committed records | `Ready` |
| exact live prepared record with its required predecessor | `AcknowledgeRequired` |
| canonical records with duplicate, gap, backward, ambiguous, two-prepared, or unattributable ordering | `Indeterminate` |
| bad format, configuration, epoch, canonical fields, digest, or checksum | `Corrupt` |

`prepareAudit()` is deliberately closed by kind. Public callers may prepare
only a pending `StopAsserted` or `StopReleased` transition. Diagnostic
acknowledgement creates its own exact `AcknowledgedDiagnostic` record.
`PuzzleSolved` is available only to the friend project coordinator in Lesson
057; a public attempt always rejects.

## Exact diagnostic acknowledgement

`prepareAcknowledge(operationId, now)` succeeds only for the exact live
`InputRecovered` or `PresentationRecovered` generation. Its returned
`PanelAcknowledgePreview` contains the panel owner token, lifecycle generation,
configuration and epoch, panel generation, operation, diagnostic generation,
and its complete audit preview.

The caller again models the prepared image write, then supplies the exact
acknowledgement in one later `update()`. A fieldwise copy is valid while the
candidate remains live. Mutation, a foreign panel, a stale generation,
consumption, stop assertion, reset, actual shutdown, or replacement
invalidates it. An envelope containing both audit acknowledgement forms
rejects atomically; neither is consumed.

The owner token is an opaque same-lifetime capability discriminator. It is not
persisted or hashed, is not a secret, and makes no same-address reconstruction
or authentication claim.

## Restart reconciliation

`shutdown()` invalidates candidates and returns the volatile panel to its
inert state. After `initialize()`, the panel has no image evidence. The caller
replays its complete copied image through `update()`; the panel classifies it
from its canonical fields.

A clean adjacent committed image becomes `Ready`. An exact attributable
prepared transaction can be completed only under the retained live
preparation contract. Torn fields, bad checksums, impossible ordering, and
unattributable prepared records fail closed as `Corrupt` or `Indeterminate`.
Reconciliation never invents a record, clears stop, acknowledges a diagnostic,
or grants actuator authority.

## Fault-desk replay

The canonical compile-only Mega sketch performs one deterministic replay over
named memory result cells:

| Stage | Predict | Observe in host tests or result cells | Interpret |
|---|---|---|---|
| initialize and empty image | blank panel, no physical ownership | successful initialization, then `PrepareRequired` | policy state was acquired; no endpoint was acquired |
| bootstrap audit | exact sequence-one prepare/write/acknowledge | committed slot, operation, kind, and `Ready` disposition | the modeled write was attributed and committed |
| navigation | one copied Next control advances one cell | selected-cell result changes with `SingleControl` | source and freshness checks admitted one command |
| presentation failure and recovery | failure is retained; exact later success is acknowledgeable | `PresentationRecovered` generation and prepared capability | presentation provenance stayed separate from primary policy |
| stop collision | stop beats acknowledgement and another presentation failure | stopped mode, unchanged selected cell, invalidated capability | precedence is envelope-based rather than call-order arbitration |
| stop audit and release | release remains stopped until its audit commits | committed `StopAsserted`, then committed `StopReleased` and Ready mode | copied deassertion alone did not erase stop history |
| shutdown, initialize, reconcile | canonical image returns deterministically | restart status and audit disposition match the supplied image | reconstruction used copied evidence, not hidden storage |

These volatile cells are the E0 non-Serial observation path. Compilation and
host replay prove deterministic packaging and policy behavior, not that a
powered board populated the cells, a display rendered them, or storage
survived power loss.

## Measured resource evidence

The canonical Mega 2560 fixture measures **18,118 bytes flash** and **1,454
bytes static SRAM**. `FaultAwareOperatorPanel` is **365 bytes** on AVR, and
the conservative synchronous stack path is **569 bytes**. All four measurements
pass the lesson's targets: 24 KiB flash, 2,560 bytes static SRAM, 640 bytes
stack, and 384 bytes object size. The hard ceilings are 28 KiB, 3,072 bytes,
768 bytes, and 512 bytes respectively.

These are compiler, linker, and conservative accounting results. They are not
runtime stack-watermark, display, storage-durability, electrical, or timing
measurements.

## E1 and E2 remain open

E1 may qualify only exact passive controls, low-voltage current-limited
indicators, and presentation hardware. It requires authoritative pin and
electrical data, supply and logic compatibility, pull policy, unpowered wiring
inspection, named observation points, failure injection, and separate proof of
resource acquisition and inactive actuator paths. Servo, relay, latch, lock,
door, and other powered actuation remain physically absent.

Any later E2 exercise is limited to a restrained no-load demonstration servo
or an inert current-limited relay-and-lamp fixture with independent physical
load-power removal and recorded bench acceptance. It must not attach to a
door, gate, lock, occupied enclosure, alarm, emergency lighting, egress route,
or life-safety system.
