# Shift register display

Status: **host verified; hardware experimental**<br>
Energy class: **E1** — USB-powered logic and resistor-limited display segments

This lesson turns three owned output pins into one readable hexadecimal display:

`glyph → segment byte → D22 data + D23 clock + D24 latch → visible segments`

`ShiftRegisterOutput` acquires the three control lines transactionally and
presents only complete latched bytes. `SevenSegmentDisplay` maps a semantic
glyph onto those bits, including common-cathode polarity and an optional
decimal point. The application asks to show a glyph; it does not reproduce the
shift timing or segment lookup table.

Predict the segment pattern first. Observe D13 for a 250 ms completed-acquisition
pulse and a 750 ms dark separator,
TP-DATA while clocking, TP-CLOCK for eight pulses, TP-LATCH for the final
presentation edge, and then the visible glyph. These signals distinguish
acquisition, serialization, and presentation: a correct data trace with the
wrong visible glyph points toward latch, resistor, segment, polarity, or
pin-order wiring.

The software-qualified circuit uses D22 for data, D23 for clock, D24 for
latch, and a TI SN74HC595N PDIP-16. The kit inventory names only a one-digit
seven-segment display; it does not establish its marking, package pinout, or
polarity. Powered construction is therefore gated on an unpowered specimen
photo/marking plus a primary datasheet or continuity map that establishes a
common-cathode display and exact a--g, decimal-point, and two common pins.
Nothing in this lesson authorizes guessing a 5161AS or another familiar
package. After that gate, every segment—including
the decimal point—has its own 470 Ω resistor; place a 100 nF bypass capacitor
at the register supply pins. A three-wire circuit does not own the register's
`/OE` pin and therefore cannot guarantee blank segments during power-up; a
later hardware refinement may add owned output enable with a pull-up.
Disconnect USB power before moving a meter or logic-analyzer clip. Stop on a
hot part, a resetting board, an output beyond the supply rails, or a glyph that
changes without a latch edge.

The bounded sketch blanks the display at 120 seconds, holds all outputs
inactive for 250 ms, then releases D22--D24 and D13. Test each released pin
one at a time with a 10 kΩ/10 kΩ midpoint fixture: remove USB, install and
inspect one fixture, repower and measure, then remove USB before changing it.
A three-or-more-channel analyzer or oscilloscope at at least 1 MHz is required
to record TP-DATA, TP-CLOCK, and TP-LATCH; its capture must show
the decoded MSB-first byte, eight clock pulses, then one latch edge. Reset
evidence separately records the unavoidable OE-low startup interval.

The bench record names the sketch commit and path, firmware artifact/hash,
source-tree state, CLI and AVR-core versions, Mega revision, USB cable and
supply, and supply current limit. With glyph 8 it
records all eight resistor values and voltage drops, seven active segment
currents plus the dark decimal-point path, their display-current sum, loaded
and unloaded 5 V rails, and total USB current. The limits are 8 mA per segment,
40 mA display total, 100 mA USB total, and a 4.75–5.25 V loaded rail.

Host tests cover acquisition rollback, exact shift order, latch timing,
polarity, every supported glyph, blanking, shutdown, and deterministic replay.
The Mega example's size is measured by the repository gate, but
physical timing, voltage, current, and visible glyph acceptance remain open
until the display specimen is qualified and a bench card is recorded.
