# Lesson 063 — inert museum-case monitor

Status: **E0 deterministic project composition; exact powered presentation,
storage, relay-lamp, and combined bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied observations and inert intents only**
Safety boundary: **no sensor, ADC channel, GPIO pin, clock, bus, display,
sounder, recorder, persistent medium, relay, lamp, mains circuit, lock,
access-control mechanism, or powered fixture**

`MuseumCaseMonitor` combines already-qualified copied evidence:

`liquid observation + thermal/radiant observation + qualified reed evidence + acknowledgement + copied audit receipt + supplied time → alarm, presentation, relay-lamp, and audit intent`

The project owns the alarm state machine and one bounded, volatile audit
transaction. It does not resample a child source, reduce delayed observations
to one simultaneous measurement, operate a display or relay, write storage,
read an RTC, preserve an artifact, secure a case, or perform a life-safety
function.

## Complete copied evidence

The configuration fixes the expected identity for all six contributing roles:

| Role | Identity retained |
|---|---|
| liquid | source, configuration revision, calibration revision, and complete Lesson 061 observation |
| thermistor | source, configuration revision, calibration revision, sequence, time, uncertainty, quality, and status |
| Digital Temperature | distinct source, configuration revision, calibration revision, categorical state, sequence, time, quality, and status |
| radiant | distinct source, configuration revision, calibration revision, raw categorical evidence, sequence, time, quality, and status |
| reed | source, configuration revision, sequence, and complete qualified `MagneticObservation` |
| acknowledgement | source, configuration revision, sequence, observation time, pressed state, and producer status |

All source IDs and revisions are nonzero, and the six roles have distinct
source IDs. The monitor also fixes separate maximum ages for liquid,
thermistor, Digital Temperature, radiant, reed, and acknowledgement evidence.
Changed or crossed identity rejects the complete frame before any alarm,
cooldown, or audit state changes.

The reed wrapper never collapses its child observation to a bare Boolean. It
retains the digital-contact source kind, raw value and level, observation
time, polarity, activation and deactivation edges, stable duration, active
state, quality, and status. A qualified active contact means case closed. A
qualified inactive contact means reed-open/case-open and raises the `Opening`
hazard regardless of electrical polarity.

## Public result

`MuseumCaseHealth` is one of `Qualifying`, `Healthy`, `Warning`, `Alarm`,
`Fault`, or `Cooldown`. `MuseumHazard` is a bit mask rather than a
highest-priority enum, so simultaneous evidence remains visible:

| Bit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Liquid` | qualified wet evidence |
| `Thermal` | qualified thermal alarm evidence |
| `Radiant` | qualified sustained radiant hazard evidence |
| `Opening` | qualified reed-open/case-open evidence |
| `Sensing` | required evidence is invalid, stale, faulted, or otherwise unqualified |
| `Recording` | audit delivery failed, expired, or exhausted |

`MuseumCaseIntent` retains owner, lifecycle, and configuration identity,
health and the complete hazard mask. It emits a grayscale-safe RGB blink code,
LCD age-or-fault visibility, sound intent, inert relay-lamp intent, and an
explicit inactive-output flag.

The caller owns `MuseumCaseResult`; `update()` fills it rather than returning
the large aggregate by value:

```cpp
Status update (
    const MuseumCaseEnvelope& envelope,
    MuseumCaseResult&         result) noexcept;
```

The result contains the current intent, at most one immutable audit intent,
and the complete status. Construction is inert. Initialization, reset, and
shutdown advance lifecycle identity so old receipts cannot cross a lifecycle.
The monitor is non-copyable and non-movable, retains no child pointer, and
allocates no heap memory.

## Alarm and cooldown precedence

Structural invalidity rejects atomically before semantic precedence. For a
valid complete frame:

1. Shutdown or lifecycle invalidity permits no active output intent.
2. Structurally valid but configured-age-stale, saturated, disconnected, or
   producer-fault evidence commits `Fault` plus `Sensing`, never `Healthy`.
3. Wet liquid, thermal alarm, radiant hazard, or reed-open evidence latches
   `Alarm`.
4. Damp liquid, thermal warning, or thermal disagreement produces `Warning`
   when no alarm or sensing fault dominates.
5. A clean first frame is immediately `Healthy`; cooldown is not an initial
   qualification stage.
6. Acknowledgement without a previously latched alarm is ineffective, and
   acknowledgement cannot clear an active hazard.
7. Only after a latched alarm's hazards have cleared does a fresh valid
   acknowledgement begin the bounded healthy cooldown.
8. A warning interrupts cooldown and reports `Warning`; a hazard or sensing
   fault relatches `Alarm` or reports `Fault`.
9. A recovered latched alarm returns to `Healthy` only at the inclusive end of
   a complete clean cooldown.

The cooldown uses supplied `TimePoint` values and wrap-safe duration rules. It
does not infer elapsed time from polling frequency. A warning cancels the
current cooldown, so a later clean recovery requires a new advancing
acknowledgement. A repeated or ineffective acknowledgement changes neither
health nor the audit decision key.

Age handling has two different boundaries. Evidence older than its configured
maximum, while still within the modular half range, is a valid observed
sensing failure and therefore commits `Fault` plus `Sensing`. A future,
backward, or exact-half-range-ambiguous time is structurally invalid and
rejects atomically instead.

`reset(now)` advances lifecycle generation, invalidates old receipts, clears
alarm, cooldown, acknowledgement history, audit attempts, and recording
faults, then returns to `Qualifying`. It does not treat a physically present
hazard as cleared: a new complete frame must qualify again. `shutdown()`
advances the lifecycle, invalidates pending audit state, and publishes
canonical inactive output intent without creating an audit record. Repeated
shutdown is idempotent.

Every not-initialized or shutdown result has sound intent false, relay-lamp
intent false, and `alarmOutputInactive` true. These are logical values, not
evidence that powered hardware is silent, dark, or de-energized.

## One immutable audit witness

The exact semantic record key is:

`{health, hazardMask}`

The first structurally valid complete frame after initialize or reset creates
one initial record for its derived decision. A clean frame is `Healthy`;
warning, alarm, sensing-fault, and accepted child-unqualified frames may
instead begin as `Warning`, `Alarm`, `Fault`, or evidence-driven `Qualifying`.
Initial `Cooldown` is unreachable because cooldown requires a previously
latched alarm, cleared hazards, and a new acknowledgement. Construction-time
and just-initialized `Qualifying` are not sensed decisions and create no
record.

After the initial record, any change to health or any hazard bit creates a new
decision record when an audit slot is available. Fresh sequences and times
with the same key do not create records. Acknowledgement creates one only when
it changes the key, such as entering cooldown. Shutdown creates none.

Each immutable `MuseumAuditIntent` contains owner token, lifecycle generation,
configuration revision, record sequence, decision observation time, health,
hazard mask, all six producer sequences, and a witness digest. Delivery
metadata—`issuedAt` and `attempt`—is deliberately outside the immutable
witness.

The digest is 32-bit FNV-1a using offset `0x811c9dc5` and prime `0x01000193`.
It folds ASCII `ADK.MUSEUM.AUDIT.V1` without a trailing NUL, followed in order
by:

1. owner token;
2. lifecycle generation;
3. configuration revision;
4. record sequence;
5. observation time;
6. health;
7. hazard mask; and
8. liquid, thermistor, Digital Temperature, radiant, reed, and acknowledgement
   sequences.

Multi-byte integers are little-endian; health and the hazard mask are one byte
each. Full witness fields are always compared. The digest never substitutes
for structural equality.

The frozen literal witness uses owner `0x01020304`, lifecycle `0x11223344`,
configuration `0x5566`, record `0x778899aa`, observation milliseconds
`0x0a0b0c0d`, `Alarm` (`0x03`), hazard mask `0x19`, and producer sequences
`{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}` in the order above. Its digest is `0x4086e509`.

## Receipt, retry, and deadline rules

Exactly one record may be outstanding. An update returns at most that
immutable intent and never calls a recorder. A copied receipt must match the
owner, lifecycle, configuration, record sequence, every witness field, digest,
and current attempt.

An accepted matching receipt retires the record. A failed receipt or deadline
loss adds the `Recording` bit, increments the bounded attempt, and reissues
the same witness on a later call. It never retries in a loop and never changes
the record sequence or digest. A missing receipt remains pending through the
inclusive deadline; the next valid tick records the loss.

At `maximumAuditAttempts`, delivery failure becomes terminal until explicit
reset. The monitor retains the immutable outstanding record, asserts
`Recording` and `Fault`, emits no further delivery attempt, and cannot become
healthy. Foreign, stale, future, crossed, wrong-digest, wrong-attempt, or
changed duplicate receipts reject atomically. A byte-identical duplicate of a
retired accepted receipt is idempotent.

The retry limit is configured from one through eight. Record sequence and
nonzero lifecycle generation fault before wrapping to zero. Generation
exhaustion leaves outputs inactive and cannot be repaired by reset.

## The dirty successor slot

A single `dirtySuccessor` slot prevents a decision change from disappearing
while a record is pending. Every valid update derives the latest complete
witness after structural receipt validation. The slot represents the net
latest decision relative to the outstanding record:

- if the current key equals the outstanding key, any dirty successor is
  cleared because the net decision returned to the frozen witness;
- if the current key equals the dirty key, the slot refreshes to the current
  complete witness, including its newest producer sequences and observation
  time; and
- otherwise the slot is replaced with the current complete witness.

The slot has no record sequence, digest, delivery attempt, or deadline until
promotion, so it cannot masquerade as a submitted record. Several pending
changes may coalesce only to that net latest successor. A same-key refresh of
the outstanding record leaves no dirty slot; a same-key refresh of an existing
successor updates its provenance without inventing another decision.

When an accepted receipt and a new decision arrive together, ordering is
fixed:

1. validate the entire envelope and receipt;
2. derive and retain the latest successor;
3. retire the accepted record;
4. increment the record sequence;
5. bind the successor to the current owner, lifecycle, and configuration;
6. compute its digest; and
7. return it immediately as attempt one.

Failed or lost receipt processing happens before successor promotion and
retains both the immutable outstanding record and the latest dirty slot.
Terminal exhaustion freezes both until reset or shutdown explicitly
invalidates them; neither action is reported as audit success.

## Deterministic collision fixture

The canonical audit collision starts with alarm record 41. While attempt one
is pending, liquid and then thermal evidence change. Attempt one fails.
Radiant and reed-open evidence then change while attempt two is pending. The
matching accepted attempt-two receipt arrives in the same update as an
acknowledgement change.

The result retires record 41 and immediately publishes record 42 with the
latest six producer sequences and latest hazard state—not either intermediate
witness. A companion trace exhausts the final attempt, proves that record 41
and its latest dirty successor remain frozen without retry, then resets and
proves both are invalidated under a new lifecycle.

The complete deterministic matrix also covers:

- first valid `Healthy`, `Warning`, `Alarm`, `Fault`, and evidence-driven
  `Qualifying` decisions in separate lifecycle fixtures, with initial
  `Cooldown` rejected as unreachable;
- every health transition and recovery;
- every individual hazard-bit addition and removal while health stays fixed;
- combined-mask changes;
- same-key sequence and age refresh;
- ineffective acknowledgement;
- ineffective acknowledgement without a latch, acknowledgement-to-cooldown
  after cleared alarm, warning interruption, and cooldown-to-healthy;
- evidence-driven `Qualifying`;
- audit acceptance, failure, inclusive deadline, retry, terminal exhaustion,
  changed duplicates, and stale/crossed receipts;
- time rollover, exact-half-range ambiguity, sequence and lifecycle
  exhaustion;
- reset, shutdown, reinitialize, and persistent faults across restart; and
- byte-identical full replay.

## Compile-only Mega replay

The canonical Mega 2560 sketch uses fixed copied fixtures, supplied time, and
named volatile result cells. It owns no sensor or output:

| Stage | Predict | Observe | Interpret |
|---:|---|---|---|
| initialize | qualifying and inactive | lifecycle, health, hazard, and inactive-output cells | policy initialized without acquiring a resource |
| healthy frame | one initial decision record | six source-sequence cells plus record key and digest | complete provenance became one audit witness |
| combined hazard | liquid, thermal, radiant, and opening bits coexist | health, full mask, presentation, sound, and inert lamp cells | no highest-priority enum erased a hazard |
| acknowledge active alarm | alarm remains latched | acknowledgement sequence and unchanged alarm cells | operator intent cannot clear an active hazard |
| cooldown | a latched alarm clears, an advancing acknowledgement starts cooldown, and no warning intervenes | start/deadline, `Cooldown`, and `Healthy` cells | supplied time controls alarm recovery, not initial qualification |
| audit failure | witness remains immutable while attempt advances | record, digest, attempt, `Recording`, and dirty-slot cells | retry metadata did not rewrite the event |
| acceptance collision | record 41 retires and record 42 appears immediately | old receipt and latest successor cells | the newest decision was not treated as already recorded |
| shutdown/restart | all output intents inactive, then fresh qualification required | lifecycle and inactive-output cells | stale receipts cannot reactivate the monitor |

Predict every named cell before the call and compare it with the deterministic
host trace. Compilation establishes packaging only. It does not establish
powered sensing, display visibility, sound, relay operation, recording
durability, preservation, security, or physical safe state.

`8192 - 1405 - 819 - 128 = 5840`, including the fixed 128-byte

`d376c43b63f42f2ed48c768d9afcaa2583d5a2b4e194a45f9a3f125694e41543`.

boundaries; their flash and static-SRAM values are not added. Compiler
measurements do not prove runtime stack depth, persistence, electrical,

## E1c, E1d, and E2 remain open

No wiring table or formal schematic is authorized at E0.

E1c independently qualifies the exact reed, RGB/LCD/status presentation, and
their non-Serial observation paths. E1d may combine the passive monitor only
after each exact source passes its own gate; it must then prove simultaneous
current and rail behavior, child ages and disagreement visibility,
transactional acquisition and rollback, shutdown, physical observation, and
separate resource-acquisition and safe-state evidence.

RTC and removable-media hardware remain separately deferred under Lessons 022
and 024. Audit intent and copied recorder receipts do not establish durable
storage, wall-clock accuracy, atomic media commit, wear behavior, or
power-loss recovery.

E2 may qualify only an exact extra-low-voltage, current-limited, inert
relay/lamp fixture with driver, flyback, optional isolation, coil/contact
ratings, independent power removal, restraint, and measured acceptance.
Mains, locks, access control, confinement, egress, preservation, and
life-safety loads are prohibited.
