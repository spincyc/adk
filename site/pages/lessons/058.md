# Lesson 058 — nonblocking multiplexed digits

Status: **E0 deterministic presentation policy; exact powered digit fixture
and bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied values and logical intent only**
Safety boundary: **no pin, timer, interrupt, digit driver, display, supply, or
powered circuit**

`MultiplexedDigitPolicy` converts one committed four-cell glyph bank into
bounded, supplied-time refresh intent:

`copied value + formatting policy + explicit time → ordered blank, segment, and digit-select intent`

The policy does not drive a display. Its E0 evidence consists of copied
transactions and named memory result cells. It does not prove electrical
polarity, visible digits, brightness, flicker, ghosting, or physical blanking.

## Fixed display contract

The logical display has four cells, numbered left to right from 0 through 3.
Each cell has segment bits `a`, `b`, `c`, `d`, `e`, `f`, `g`, and `dp`.
Lesson 010 remains the canonical source for seven-segment glyph encoding.

Values from 0 through 9,999 render numerically. A larger value renders four
dashes, clears every decimal point, and reports overflow; it is never wrapped
or truncated. Leading zeros may be shown or blanked, but zero always retains
one visible zero. Decimal-mask bit zero names the rightmost cell.

Segment polarity and digit-select polarity are configured independently.
Common-anode or common-cathode metadata does not infer a future external
digit-driver topology.

## One bounded refresh transaction

Each due `refresh(now)` call emits exactly one three-stage transaction:

1. all digit selects inactive while the prior segment levels remain;
2. all digit selects inactive while the next segment levels are applied; and
3. exactly one digit select active.

Digits advance left to right. The default service period is 1 ms and the
maximum accepted service gap is 4 ms. An early call emits nothing. A due call
advances one digit, anchors timing at the supplied `now`, and never loops to
catch up.

The gap limit is inclusive: 4 ms remains valid and the next tick faults. A
longer gap latches `RefreshLost`, publishes blank intent, and requires an
explicit reset. Equal timestamps are idempotent after the first service.
Backward time and the exact unsigned half-range ambiguity reject atomically.

Initialization establishes blank intent and arms one explicit first-service
exception: the first same-time refresh emits digit zero's complete
transaction. `reset(now)` keeps immutable configuration, invalidates old
previews, clears the frame, and returns to that blank armed state.
`shutdown()` invalidates previews and is idempotent; refresh remains inert
until a later successful initialization.

## Atomic frame publication

Formatting follows `preview()`, `canCommit()`, and `commit()`. A preview is
bound to its policy owner, lifecycle, configuration, and base generation.
Foreign, stale, altered, or consumed previews cannot commit.

A successful commit changes the desired glyph bank without tearing the bank
currently being scanned. The new bank becomes active only when refresh next
reaches digit zero. Every bank retains its local generation and the
caller-supplied source snapshot sequence.

The policy is inert after construction, non-copyable, and non-movable. It
owns no caller pointer, reads no clock, allocates no heap storage, invokes no
callback, retries nothing, and performs bounded work on every call.

## Compile-only replay

The canonical Mega 2560 sketch uses named volatile result cells rather than
Serial as its observation path:

| Stage | Predict | Observe | Interpret |
|---:|---|---|---|
| initialize | blank intent and digit zero armed | lifecycle, blank, and generation cells | policy state initialized; no endpoint acquired |
| first service | one complete digit-zero transaction | three ordered stage cells | blank-before-select ordering is explicit |
| numeric frame | committed value waits for digit zero | desired and active bank generations | the logical frame swaps without tearing |
| overflow | four dashes and no decimal points | glyph and decimal-mask cells | range failure remains visible |
| late service | refresh fault and blank intent | `RefreshLost` and inactive-select cells | missed service fails dark in policy |
| shutdown | no later transaction | shutdown and result cells | logical activity stopped; no physical blanking claim |

Predict the complete copied values before each call, inspect the result cells,
and compare them with the deterministic host trace. Those cells establish E0
software evidence only; compilation does not prove that a Mega executed the
sketch.

## Deterministic proof

Host tests cover every canonical glyph, leading-zero and decimal combination,
all four segment/select polarity combinations, exact three-stage ordering,
digit sequence, frame-swap boundary, early and due service, refresh loss,
equal timestamps, rollover, half-range rejection, lifecycle transitions,
preview ownership and generation collisions, shutdown, and byte-identical
replay.

The exact AVR resource gate records flash, static SRAM, object size,
conservative synchronous stack, residual SRAM, and toolchain fingerprints.
The reviewed no-LTO maximum measures 6,044 bytes flash, 759 bytes static
SRAM, 187 bytes synchronous stack, and a 59-byte policy object; every target
and hard gate passes.
Those measurements are compiler evidence, not runtime stack-watermark,
electrical, optical, or physical evidence.

## E1a remains open

E0 claims zero hardware resources. Resource-acquisition evidence is therefore
“no resources requested,” while safe-state evidence is the copied blank
snapshot and inactive-select intent. These are separate policy observations.

E1a may begin only after the exact four-digit specimen, polarity, pin map,
segment and digit drivers, and resistor network are identified. It must use
one resistor per segment and a rated digit-driver topology rather than routing
aggregate common current through a Mega pin. Its authoritative formal
schematic and bench record must cover:

- peak segment current, multiplex duty, and per-port sourcing and sinking;
- digit-driver peak and continuous ratings;
- worst-case simultaneous segments and loaded supply current;
- refresh waveform, brightness, ghosting, reset, and observed blanking;
- transactional resource acquisition and rollback; and
- separate physical-power removal and unpowered wiring inspection.

Until those gates pass, do not connect the E0 policy to a physical display.
Software blank intent, reset, shutdown, and destruction are not proof that a
powered display is dark.
