# Lesson 037 — contact dynamics

Status: **host verified; reference-fixture bench work open**

Energy class: **E1 passive contact fixture** (the absent-hardware policy trace
is E0)

This lesson converts one timestamped active-low contact trace into qualified
attack and release evidence:

`raw level → qualified contact → attack event → release event`

The canonical reference is the C&K/Littelfuse `RB-220-07A R` rolling-ball
contact. Mega 5 V feeds `TP-C` through an external 10 kΩ pull-up; the contact
connects `TP-C` to ground. The nominal closed current is 0.5 mA. Configure
`DigitalInput` with `Pull::None` and `ContactDynamics` with
`activeLevel = Level::Low`.

This documented reference circuit enables compilation and deterministic
trace work. It is not a physical-verification claim. Before power, confirm the
delivered part number, condition, terminal continuity, installed closure
orientation, resistor value, and literal wiring. Powered characterization and
the bench acceptance record remain open.

## Predict, observe, interpret

Three resistor-limited LEDs make evidence visible without Serial:

| Witness | Mega pin | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| raw LED | D30 | the sampled contact is closed |
| accepted LED | D31 | a 150 ms witness for one accepted attack |
| ready/fault LED | D32 | acquired and healthy; dark on stuck/fault |

`TP-C` is D22 measured relative to Mega ground. Before power, predict that an
open switch leaves `TP-C` near 5 V and closure brings it near 0 V. During a
qualified closure, compare `TP-C`, the raw LED, then the accepted LED. A brief
accepted-LED pulse means policy accepted one attack; it does not measure force,
impact energy, orientation, damage, or safety.

The LEDs being dark before `setup()` does not prove acquisition. A successful
start first acquires D22 and all three outputs. Shutdown then releases every
pin to high impedance; bench acceptance must measure that safe state
separately.

## Contact policy

`ContactDynamics` owns no GPIO. It receives copied `ContactSample` values and
retains a stable `ContactObservation`. Qualification and release each require
continuous evidence. Refractory time suppresses repeated attacks, while a
held contact eventually reports `StuckActive`. Source and timing faults create
no event and require `reset()`.

Natural 32-bit rollover is valid. An apparent jump at or beyond the unsigned
half-range is a timing fault. An identical same-time sample is idempotent;
changed evidence at the same time is invalid.

The canonical sketch uses 30 ms qualification, 20 ms release, 80 ms
refractory, and a 2 s stuck-active boundary. It runs for at most 120 s and
shuts down on any acquisition, sample-policy, or LED-output failure.

## Absent-hardware trace path

No contact is needed to verify the policy. Replay the checked-in deterministic
tests and the active-low trace below:

| Time | Level | Expected evidence |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 ms | High | idle |
| 10 ms | Low | candidate starts |
| 20 ms | High | chatter cancels candidate |
| 30 ms | Low | candidate restarts |
| 60 ms | Low | accepted attack |
| 70 ms | High | release candidate |
| 90 ms | High | qualified release, 30 ms width |
| 100 ms | Low | candidate during refractory |
| 130 ms | Low | suppressed attack |

Retain every status and snapshot field after every row, then replay from
reset. Field-for-field equality is deterministic evidence; comparing raw
object bytes is not.

Use a separate shortened E0 held-contact trace with `stuckActive = 120 ms`:
submit `(0, High)`, `(10, Low)`, `(40, Low)`, `(160, Low)`, `(170, High)`,
and `(190, High)`. Predict acceptance at 40 ms, `StuckActive` exactly at
160 ms, and release with a 150 ms pulse width at 190 ms. The physical sketch
deliberately uses the longer 2 s boundary.

## Safety and acceptance boundary

Remove USB power before wiring, moving the contact, placing a meter probe, or
changing a resistor. Use only the named reference part or a separately
qualified substitution.
Do not substitute mercury, a cracked or unknown capsule, a bare piezo, or an
unidentified module. Stop on uncertain identity, damage, unexpected
continuity, the wrong resistor, an unresolved ground, or any predicted
current/voltage outside the documented bounds. Do not infer ratings or wiring
from appearance. Measure
the actual rail and resistor tolerance before claiming that worst-case closed
current remains at or below the manufacturer’s 1 mA maximum.

Host compilation, a generated PDF, and trace replay are not bench acceptance.
The open card must record the delivered specimen, supply, instruments,
prediction, `TP-C` levels, each LED resistor and current bound, LED
observations, closed current, shutdown state, and interpretation.
