# Lesson 043 — copied inertial observations

Status: **E0 synthetic replay; powered adapter and bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied-sample policy**
Safety boundary: **fixed fixture values and host-result records only**

`InertialObservationPolicy` validates one supplied six-axis sample while
preserving the evidence needed to interpret it:

`copied values + provenance + time + sequence → current, stale, saturated, or invalid`

The policy owns no sensor, bus, endpoint, callback, or clock. The caller
supplies values already expressed in fixed-point units, the observation time,
and the policy time. Validation does not filter, rotate, calibrate, integrate,
or combine samples.

## Copied values and provenance

Every accepted sample retains:

- acceleration in `AccelerationMicroG`, represented by the
  `accelerationMicroG` vector;
- angular rate in `AngularRateMilliDegreesPerSecond`, represented by the
  `angularRateMilliDegreesPerSecond` vector;
- `InertialSampleSequence`, represented by `sequence`;
- source kind, model, and nonzero source ID;
- configuration and `CalibrationRevision` values;
- declared acceleration and angular-rate ranges;
- the accepted payload's `sample.dataReady`, saturation, timestamp, and
  complete `Status` evidence;
- `latestDataReady`, which reports the latest valid readiness poll for that
  same source domain and sequence; and
- the configured maximum age and freshness-contract revision used to derive
  quality.

These are copied values, not measurements made by ADK. The vectors gain their
units from the fields that contain them; `InertialVector` is not a unit-free
vector-math interface.

Changing the source kind, model, source ID, either declared range,
configuration revision, or calibration revision starts a new sequence domain.
The policy does not claim that records from different domains are equivalent.

## Quality and status precedence

| Evidence | Published quality | Published status |
|---|---|---|
| producer reports a failure | `Invalid` | complete producer status |
| malformed source, timing, sequence, range, or saturation contract | `Invalid` | non-OK validation status |
| valid value at a declared axis range | `Saturated` | `Ok` |
| valid value older than `maximumAge`, or no new data-ready sample | `Stale` | `Ok` |
| valid, ready value at or inside the age boundary | `Current` | `Ok` |

A producer failure makes the numeric payload untrustworthy and dominates
range, saturation, and age classification. Structural invalidity dominates
staleness. Saturation wins over staleness because the copied value has already
lost bounded measurement meaning. `Stale` and `Saturated` are explicit
qualities, not transport errors.

Age equal to `maximumAge` remains current; one tick beyond it is stale.
Repeating the complete same sample at a later policy time is idempotent sample
evidence, but recomputes age so a current observation can become stale. A
same-domain, same-sequence poll whose sole change is
`dataReady: true → false` is the one allowed delta-zero change. It retains the
accepted sample byte-for-byte, sets `latestDataReady` false, publishes
`Stale`, recomputes age from the accepted timestamp, and reports no sequence
gap. Any other changed sample at the same sequence is invalid. Forward modular
sequences record skipped samples in `sequenceGap`; an ambiguous half-range
jump or regression is rejected.

Keeping both readiness fields is intentional: `sample.dataReady` describes
the retained payload that produced the axes, provenance, timestamp, and
sequence, while `latestDataReady` describes the newest valid readiness
evidence without rewriting that payload. A newly accepted ready sample or an
exact ready replay sets `latestDataReady` true; reset clears it to false.

## Synthetic replay

The canonical compile-only sketch replays fixed level, stationary-gravity,
hand-tilt, saturation, stale, and producer-failure frames. For each frame it
copies quality, status, age, sequence gap, provenance, and all six axes into
named memory result cells.

| Fixture | Predict | Observe | Interpret |
|---|---|---|---|
| stationary gravity | `Current` | zero sequence gap and copied +Z value | a valid copied record, not physical level |
| hand-tilt values | `Current` | all axes and revisions remain intact | validation does not derive orientation |
| acceleration at its declared bound | `Saturated` | `Ok` status and saturation provenance | the numeric value has bounded measurement loss |
| age one tick beyond the maximum | `Stale` | preserved sample with increased age | old evidence is explicit, not silently current |
| same-sequence not-ready poll | `Stale` | retained `sample.dataReady == true`, `latestDataReady == false`, zero gap | readiness changed without fabricating a payload or sequence |
| producer hardware failure | `Invalid` | the producer status is retained | no numeric classification is trusted |

Run the deterministic host test, retain every named result after every frame,
reset, and replay the same inputs. Field-for-field equality is deterministic
evidence. Comparing padding bytes or using `memcmp` is not.

The named memory cells are the non-Serial observation path for this E0 lesson.
The Mega sketch is a packaging artifact: compile it, but do not execute it on
a powered Mega or inspect it through a powered hardware debugger as E0
evidence. Initialization acquires no hardware resource, and reset performs no
hardware action; those separate results do not establish a physical safe
state.

## Powered-work gate

No wiring table, I2C address, register transaction, pin assignment, or formal
schematic is published here. `Mpu6050Adapter` and `Qmi8658Adapter` source kinds
are rejected as unsupported in the E0 policy so an unqualified physical claim
cannot enter accidentally.

An MPU-6050 and a QMI8658 require separate future adapters, exact delivered
specimen identification, carrier and chip markings, primary electrical and
register documentation, voltage and level qualification, and independent
bench evidence. A retail kit name does not establish any of those facts. In
particular, QMI8658 family identity cannot be inferred safely from one shared
identity value.

Compilation, host replay, this page, and the printable lesson do not prove a
powered sensor, conversion scale, calibration, mounting direction, accuracy,
noise, drift, timing, voltage, current, or physical safe state. Lesson 043
makes no navigation, position, stabilization, or motion-tracking claim.
