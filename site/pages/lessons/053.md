# Lesson 053 — known local infrared emission

Status: **E0 inert policy replay; exact emitter fixture and bench acceptance
open**

Energy class: **E0 symbolic intent only**
Safety boundary: **closed firmware-authored catalog; no pin, timer, carrier,
emitter, receiver, or optical power path**

`KnownIrEmissionPolicy` turns one locally authored symbolic command and
caller-supplied microsecond time into a bounded carrier-envelope intent:

`LocalIrCodeId + explicit time → transactional envelope intent`

The policy publishes only `CarrierOn`, `CarrierOff`, or `Inactive` memory
intent. It does not generate a carrier, toggle a pin, illuminate an emitter,
operate an appliance, or prove that a physical output is dark.

## A closed catalog, not a learned remote

The public request surface accepts only one of four fixed symbols:

| `LocalIrCodeId` | Harmless local meaning |
|---|---|
| `StationPing` | ask the adjacent lesson fixture to identify itself |
| `StationReady` | report that the local fixture is ready |
| `StationCancel` | cancel the local fixture's pending exchange |
| `StationAcknowledge` | acknowledge the local fixture's expected response |

These entries are immutable firmware data. Their catalog revision and digest
cover every semantic entry field and appear in every preview and snapshot.
There is no operation for adding, editing, importing, learning, or inferring a
catalog entry at runtime. The canonical replay checks the exact expected
revision and digest before accepting any fixture work, then records the same
exact comparison beside every presented result. A nonmatching identity stops
the replay; it is not treated as a compatible or approximately known catalog.

No public constructor, configuration, request, preview, helper, or conversion
accepts a `PulseFrame`, `InfraredFrame`, `CapturedIrView`, raw address and
command, duration array, waveform, or arbitrary bytes. Lesson 052 evidence
therefore has no type-directed path into transmission. Unknown, malformed, and
repeat captures can be inspected, but they cannot become transmit authority.

## Prepare, check, then commit

`prepare(codeId, transactionId, now)` validates the symbolic request and
reserves exactly one private candidate. The returned
`KnownIrEmissionPreview` is bound to:

- the policy instance, configuration revision, and instance epoch;
- the current policy and candidate generations;
- the nonzero transaction ID and selected local code;
- the immutable catalog revision and digest;
- a candidate digest; and
- the exact proposed start and completion times.

Only that retained candidate can be committed. A foreign, reconstructed,
changed, stale, consumed, or cancelled preview rejects. A second prepare while
the candidate is reserved reports busy.

`canCommit(preview, now)` and `commit(preview, now)` require
`now == preview.startAt`. A delayed commit does not backdate or shift the
envelope; the caller must prepare a new candidate for the later start.
Successful commit is one atomic transition from the private candidate to the
active transaction.

## Direct timing without catch-up bursts

The policy derives the single intent that is true at the supplied
`MicrosecondTimePoint`. It does not iterate carrier cycles, scan every prior
mark and space, queue transitions, or replay missed work.

Calling `update(now)` late publishes the phase appropriate at `now`. At or
after `completeAt`, it publishes `Inactive` and `Complete`; it never reenacts
an interval that the caller missed. Exact boundary, repeated-time, rollover,
regressing-time, and half-range cases are deterministic. A regressing or
ambiguous time rejects atomically without changing the prior snapshot.

This is envelope scheduling, not carrier synthesis. A future qualified
endpoint must independently own the carrier frequency, duty cycle, output
polarity, timer and pin claims, and electrically inactive state.

## Cancellation and shutdown

Before commit, `cancel(preview, now)` must identify the exact reserved
owner, configuration, instance epoch, policy and candidate generations,
transaction, code, catalog, candidate digest, and start time. It records
`CancelledBeforeCommit`, invalidates that capability, and never publishes
`CarrierOn`. Repeating that same exact cancellation is idempotent; a copied
preview with any changed field rejects without disturbing the reservation.

After commit, `cancel(transactionId, now)` must identify the exact active
transaction. It immediately publishes `Inactive` memory intent and records
`CancelledActive`. Repeating cancellation for the same terminal identity is
idempotent; a foreign or stale identity rejects without mutation.

At a shared timestamp, a composing coordinator consumes cancellation before
commit, update, or application of intent. This arbitration makes cancellation
dominate a due transition regardless of event arrival order. Direct callers
that omit that coordination cannot claim call-order-independent dominance.

`shutdown()` also publishes `Inactive` and distinguishes prepared work from an
active transaction in its terminal record. Reinitialization advances the
policy generation, so an old preview cannot become valid again. Cancellation
and shutdown are software evidence only: neither is an emergency stop,
physical power removal, nor proof that an emitter is electrically off.

## Secret-handshake replay

The canonical compile-only Mega sketch uses a fixed “secret handshake”
sequence. `setup()` acquires the inert policy, checks the immutable catalog
identity, and starts idle. `loop()` observes a symbolic local request and
supplied time, decides through prepare, commit, update, and cancellation, then
actuates only named memory result cells.

| Replay stage | Predict | Observe in executable host tests | Interpret |
|---|---|---|---|
| initialize | idle, inactive policy | `Idle` with `Inactive` intent | no physical resource was acquired |
| prepare `StationPing` | one reserved candidate | `Prepared`, matching transaction and catalog digest | a symbol selected closed firmware data |
| exact-time commit | transaction starts atomically | `Active` and the boundary's envelope intent | authorization was not backdated |
| representative boundaries | one intent is derived directly | `CarrierOn` or `CarrierOff`, current repeat index, stable deadline | no missed transitions were replayed |
| precommit cancellation | no active interval begins | `CancelledBeforeCommit`, `Inactive` | the retained capability was invalidated |
| active cancellation | intent becomes inactive at once | `CancelledActive`, matching terminal transaction | cancellation dominated policy progression |
| late update | completed work stays inactive | `Complete`, `Inactive` | lateness did not create a catch-up burst |
| shutdown and restart | old preview remains stale | shutdown attribution, then a newer generation | restart did not revive prior authority |

The named result cells are the E0 non-Serial observation path. Compilation and
host replay prove packaging and deterministic policy behavior; they do not
prove that a powered Mega populated the cells or that infrared light was
emitted.

## Measured resource evidence

The current canonical Mega 2560 ELF measures **4,854 bytes flash** and
**276 bytes static SRAM**. This supersedes the earlier pre-enhancement
4,188-byte / 336-byte observation: the sketch now records the exact catalog
check and expanded replay evidence, and the linked ELF is the authoritative
final measurement.

On AVR, `KnownIrEmissionPolicy` is **74 bytes**, within its 96-byte target and
128-byte hard ceiling. The conservative live stack path, including the
interrupt reserve, is approximately **155 bytes**. Static SRAM plus that
allowance leaves about **7,740 bytes** of Mega SRAM, comfortably beyond the
standalone lesson's required margin. These are compiler, linker, and
conservative accounting results; they are not runtime stack-watermark,
timing-latency, carrier-output, or electrical measurements.

## E1 remains open

No exact emitter is selected, and the authorized Elegoo inventory does not
establish that its kit contains one. E1 must separately qualify the exact
emitter, driver, resistor, supply, carrier timer/channel/pin, receiver,
observation path, optical geometry, default-off behavior, and bench
acceptance. Current limiting and bounded duty reduce exposure; they do not
establish eye safety. This lesson makes **no eye-safety claim**.

Until that qualification exists, there is no wiring table, formal schematic,
powered procedure, carrier-frequency evidence, radiant-output claim, or
permission to point an emitter at an unknown device.
