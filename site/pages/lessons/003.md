# Button and reaction timer

Status: **host verified; Mega hardware acceptance pending**

The E1 integration project combines an active-low D22 button, an external D8
cue LED through 330 Ω, deterministic debounce, explicit time, and a
hardware-neutral state engine. D13 provides a separate 250 ms
resource-acquisition pulse. A saved timestamped input trace reproduces every
transition and result.

```cpp
const adk::TimePoint now (millis ());

button.update (now);
timer.update  (now, button);
led.set       (timer.snapshot ().ledOn);
```

The canonical sketch and published circuit both use D8 for the cue; D13 is
acquisition evidence only. Holding D22 for three seconds reaches controlled
shutdown: D8 and D13 are driven low briefly and then D8, D13, and D22 are
released to high impedance. USB power removal remains the emergency stop.
Physical release evidence uses measured 10 kΩ midpoint bias on one output at
a time and the named DMM weak path on released D22; every fixture change occurs
with USB removed. The exact board D13 circuit must be included in its predicted
range. The card also records a no-press timeout trial.

The PDF provides the exact D22/D8 bench circuit, named TP-BUTTON, TP-CUE, and
TP-ACQ test points, pencil orientation plate, timing diagrams, replay
worksheets, false-start experiments, complete specimen/tool/operator/reviewer
identity fields, and prediction/observation/interpretation rows. No physical
verification is claimed until that card is reviewed and executed.
