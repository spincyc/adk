# Make a climate light

Prerequisites: **Lessons 001–012**

Build a DHT11 sensor that earns a green light only when a complete, current
frame passes timing, checksum, and range rules. It starts with a blue pulse;
green is the satisfying finish. No Serial monitor or learner test sheet is
needed.

## Use the exact build

The printable lesson's pencil plates assume:

- an Arduino Mega 2560;
- a labeled DHT11 module whose exact revision documents 5 V operation and
  identifies its `+`, `S`/DATA, `-`, and whether a DATA pull-up is fitted;
- a documented common-cathode RGB LED; and
- one 330 Ω resistor per LED channel.

D22 connects to S/DATA. Module `+` connects to 5V and `-` to GND. D5, D6, and
D7 drive the red, green, and blue anodes. Do not infer the three-pin order:
module revisions differ. Add no external pull-up when the identified module
already has one. Unplug USB before changing wiring.

## The quick progression

1. Locate D22, D5–D7, 5V, and GND on the actual Mega headers.
2. Wire only the DHT11 and its pull-up.
3. Add the resistor-limited RGB light.
4. Upload the sketch: blue pulses while starting, then a steady valid color
   appears after the first accepted frame.
5. Breathe gently across the vents from several inches away. The steady color
   moves through green (under 55% RH), cyan (55–69.9%), and amber (70% or
   higher), then returns as room air replaces your breath.
6. For one reversible surprise, unplug USB, lift only DATA, and power again.
   Two red pulses show that the sensor cannot bluff its way to green. Restore
   DATA to recover.

| Lamp | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Blue pulse | Waiting for the first eligible frame |
| Steady green, cyan, or amber | A complete frame is valid and fresh; color shows its humidity band |
| Two red pulses | The sensor transaction timed out |
| Three red pulses | Checksum or decoded-value validation failed |
| Amber | The last good sample became old |

The steady valid color validates the digital frame, not physical calibration.
The implementation always bounds wire waits, releases DATA after requesting,
and turns the indicator off during shutdown. Deterministic host checks cover
faults, replay, rollover, lifecycle, and cleanup behind the scenes.
