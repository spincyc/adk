# Lesson 040 — reflective and interrupted light

Status: **E0 host verified; powered fixture and bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied-sample policies**
Safety boundary: **synthetic optical evidence only**

Two deliberately separate policies turn supplied samples into stable evidence:

`reflective ADC sample → calibration-bound marker state`

`beam level sample → qualified interruption edge`

`ReflectiveObservationPolicy` and `BeamObservationPolicy` own no pin, endpoint,
emitter, timer, callback, or clock. The caller supplies every sample and
timestamp. A family name such as photo-resistor, tracking, avoidance, or
photo-interrupter does not identify an electrical circuit.

## Reflective evidence

A `ReflectiveSample` retains its source ID, calibration revision, timestamp,
raw ADC count, and complete `Status`. The policy checks a configured qualified
range, maps the two distinct references into 0–1000 with widened integer
arithmetic, and clamps the result.

Activation and release thresholds form a polarity-aware hysteresis band.
Continuous evidence must satisfy the configured dwell before the state changes.
The published `markerActive` state is polarity-independent, so later course
policy does not need to know whether darker or lighter evidence means active.

The raw count always remains visible. Out-of-range evidence can prove only that
the supplied count left the configured range. It cannot diagnose an open wire,
short, floating input, ambient change, surface material, distance, or crosstalk.

## Interrupted-light evidence

A `BeamSample` retains the same provenance vocabulary plus its raw digital
level. The policy maps one configured level to `interrupted`, qualifies
interruption and restoration with independent dwell times, and publishes
one-update edge events.

Short pulses, bounce, and chatter remain visible in the supplied trace but do
not become qualified edges until their dwell boundary. A digital level is only
copied evidence; it does not prove a safe emitter, a physical beam, or an exact
module.

## Time and fault rules

Time validation wins before source interpretation. An identical same-time
sample is idempotent. Changed same-time evidence, backward apparent time, or an
exact-or-greater unsigned half-range jump reports `TimingFault` without partial
mutation. Events clear on the next later update. Reset clears qualification,
candidates, and events while leaving an initialized policy ready for its next
sample.

Source status, malformed levels, degenerate calibration, below-range, and
above-range evidence remain distinguishable. Equal numeric source IDs never
alias across reflective and interrupted source kinds.

## Predict, observe, interpret

1. Predict that a slow synthetic darkening ramp must cross the activation
   threshold and remain there for the whole dwell before `markerActive`.
2. Observe the raw count, normalized result, supplied timestamps around the
   threshold crossing, and one-update activation event. Derive the dwell from
   the input trace; candidate state is intentionally not public.
3. Reverse the ramp. Interpret no release until the separate release threshold
   and dwell both succeed.
4. Replay a short interrupted pulse, then one exactly equal to the configured
   dwell. Predict suppression of the first and one qualified edge for the
   second.
5. Change a sample at the same timestamp. Predict `TimingFault` and no partially
   updated observation.
6. Reset. Confirm that prior events and candidates disappear before replaying
   the same trace byte-for-byte.

The synthetic trace itself is the non-Serial observation path for this E0
lesson. The eventual powered circuit must add named raw, event, and ready/fault
indicators and separately prove resource acquisition and shutdown state.

## Powered-work gate

No powered adapter, wiring table, pin assignment, or formal schematic is
published here. Before any is added, one exact specimen must establish
markings, pin order, supply, output rails, polarity, pull-ups, emitter behavior,
current, and an authoritative electrical source. `Laser Emit` remains excluded
and unpowered.

Compilation, deterministic replay, and this documentation do not prove a
physical optical module, voltage, current, distance, reflectance, ambient
rejection, crosstalk, or bench behavior.
