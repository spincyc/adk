# Make a servo pointer move

Prerequisites: **Lessons 001–016**

Build three prediction lights, give a paper pointer low/middle/high targets,
then make the pointer follow the lights. Each press advances through center,
low, middle, and high.

![Four pencil plates: make a paper pointer card; wire D30–D32 marker LEDs on a breadboard; connect D44 signal and common ground while servo positive stays open; then compare the low, middle, and high markers with the moving pointer.](../downloads/assets/017-bounded-servo-pencil.png)

## Build it in four quick stages

1. With all power off, cut a light paper arrow and draw LOW, MIDDLE, and HIGH
   around a generous clear arc.
2. Wire three breadboard LEDs with their own 220 Ω or larger resistors. The
   Mega side header is paired: use D30, D31, and D32—not three holes that merely
   look adjacent. Add a button from D22 to GND.
3. Upload with servo positive disconnected. D13 and the middle marker appear.
   Each press changes the prediction light. Act it out with the loose arrow.
4. Only after the exact servo and supply are admitted, connect D44 to signal,
   join common ground, and deliberately close the separate servo-positive
   switch. Watch the prediction become motion.

| Part | Mega connection | Other connection |
|---|---|---|
| Button | D22 | GND; internal pull-up |
| Low marker | D30 | resistor → LED → GND |
| Middle marker | D31 | resistor → LED → GND |
| High marker | D32 | resistor → LED → GND |
| Ready | built-in D13 | no external wire |
| Servo signal / TP-S | D44/OC5C | identified servo signal |
| Common reference | Mega GND | servo-supply negative |
| Servo positive | no Mega connection | rated supply through reachable disconnect |

## The short moving-parts rule

Use an **identified servo**, its **rated current-limited external supply**, a
**common ground**, and a **physical disconnect in servo positive**. The servo
does not take power from Mega 5 V, VIN, USB 5 V, or an I/O pin. Fit only the
soft paper pointer—no load, linkage, latch, spring, hard stop, or pinch point.

Choose connector order, voltage, current limit, and pulse range from primary
documentation for the servo actually in hand. Visual resemblance does not
prove it is an SG90. If identity or ratings are unknown, enjoy the complete
marker-light stage and leave servo positive disconnected.

Open servo-positive power before wiring, upload, reset, or touching the
pointer. Open it immediately for unexpected motion, chatter, stall, heat,
odor, supply sag, current limiting, or contact with the marked arc.

## What runs

The example validates its fixed-size configuration record, transactionally
owns D44 and Timer5, and starts at center. Presses request 1500, 1000, 1500,
then 2000 μs. The matching LED says what the program requested; the pointer is
the visible physical answer.

```cpp
const uint16_t position = positions[nextPosition];

if (!commandPosition (position))
{
    stopLogic ();
    return;
}
```

An out-of-range command is rejected without changing the prior bounded intent.
A resource or waveform failure revokes command admission, disables Timer5,
makes D44 high impedance, and shuts down the indicators. After two minutes the
sketch performs the same tidy logic shutdown. That does **not** remove external
servo power; open the physical disconnect yourself.

## If the picture does not match

- D13 dark: leave servo power open and inspect the USB-only button/LED build.
- Wrong marker: compare D30/D31 and D32/D33 paired positions with the drawing.
- Markers work but the pointer does not: open power, then verify the exact
  connector order, common ground, supply setting, and physical disconnect.
- Pointer moves backward: open power and correct the card direction or admitted
  calibration. Never force the horn.
- Buzz, chatter, heat, or supply sag: open servo-positive power immediately.
