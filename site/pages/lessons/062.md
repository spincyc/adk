# Lesson 062 — qualifying copied thermal and radiant observations

Status: **E0 deterministic copied-evidence policy; exact thermistor, Digital
Temperature, and radiant fixtures and bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied values only**
Safety boundary: **no ADC channel, GPIO pin, bus, sensor, divider, threshold
module, radiant stimulus, clock, display, relay, flame, heater, laser, or
powered circuit**

`ThermalRadiantObservationPolicy` qualifies one complete envelope containing
three deliberately different sources:

`converted thermistor evidence + categorical Digital Temperature evidence + categorical radiant evidence + supplied time → one attributed observation`

The thermistor producer supplies an already-converted temperature and its
uncertainty. The unidentified Digital Temperature source supplies only a
categorical threshold state. The radiant source supplies raw, unitless
threshold evidence. The policy does not read any device, perform thermistor
conversion, identify a transport, infer temperature from the two categorical
sources, or detect flame.

![a pencil drawing places three unidentified physical specimens behind a
locked E1b gate, while three copied cards---one converted thermistor, one
Digital Temperature threshold, and one radiant threshold---enter a pure
policy on the E0 side](../downloads/assets/062-evidence-boundary-pencil.png)

## Three roles, three contracts

The three source IDs must be nonzero and distinct. Each copied source also
carries its own nonzero configuration and calibration revision, producer
sequence, observation time, saturation flag, and complete producer `Status`.
One source's health cannot repair or authenticate another source.

| Role | E0 value meaning | Meaning it does not acquire |
|---|---|---|
| thermistor | producer-converted milli-degrees Celsius with uncertainty | divider topology, resistance, curve, ADC reference, or physical accuracy |
| Digital Temperature | `Below` or `AtOrAbove` its producer's threshold | temperature units, protocol, 18B20 identity, threshold value, or pinout |
| radiant | raw value plus `Below` or `AtOrAbove` threshold state | temperature, irradiance, wavelength, flame presence, or life-safety detection |

Only the thermistor path carries temperature units at E0. “Digital
Temperature” remains an inventory-family label, not permission to treat the
source as an 18B20. Lessons 064–066 retain the separate single-wire and listed
18B20 work.

![three pencil-drawn cards share fields for source, configuration,
calibration, sequence, observation time, saturation, and producer status;
only the thermistor card has milli-Celsius and uncertainty, while the other
two have raw and threshold state](../downloads/assets/062-three-source-envelope-pencil.png)

## Fixed public contract

`ConvertedThermalSample` copies the thermistor source identity, revisions,
sequence, observation time, milli-Celsius value, uncertainty, saturation flag,
and producer status.

`CategoricalThresholdSample` copies the corresponding identity and ordering
evidence, a raw value, a fixed `Below` or `AtOrAbove` state, saturation, and
producer status. The Digital Temperature and radiant roles use separate
instances; their raw values are not comparable.

`ThermalRadiantConfig` fixes ordered warning and alarm temperatures, maximum
source age, and the radiant pulse and sustained-duration boundaries. The pulse
maximum must be nonzero and shorter than the sustained minimum. Every duration
is below the modular half range.

`ThermalRadiantObservation` returns the complete copied envelope, independent
ages, `ThermalQuality`, `RadiantQuality`, two hazard booleans, and a
structural/lifecycle `Status`.

The policy surface is deliberately small:

```cpp
explicit ThermalRadiantObservationPolicy (
    const ThermalRadiantConfig& config) noexcept;

Status initialize () noexcept;
void   reset      () noexcept;
Status update     (
    TimePoint now, const ThermalRadiantEnvelope& envelope) noexcept;

ThermalRadiantObservation snapshot    () const noexcept;
bool                      initialized () const noexcept;
```

Construction is inert. The policy copies its configuration and accepted
evidence, retains no caller pointer, allocates no heap memory, invokes no
callback, owns no endpoint, and reads no clock. Reset clears ordering and
radiant-candidate history and publishes an unqualified observation while
leaving an initialized policy ready for a fresh sequence.

![pencil number lines show temperature uncertainty intervals ending below
warning, touching warning, lying between warning and alarm, and touching
alarm; equality moves conservatively into the higher quality band](../downloads/assets/062-uncertainty-intervals-pencil.png)

## Conservative thermal classification

The copied thermistor value and uncertainty define explanatory endpoints with
widened signed arithmetic:

`lower = milliCelsius - uncertaintyMilliCelsius`

`upper = milliCelsius + uncertaintyMilliCelsius`

The policy computes only the upper bound because it alone controls the
conservative temperature class. Tests independently check that the explanatory
lower endpoint remains representable at signed extremes:

| Interval result | Thermistor class |
|---|---|
| `upper < warning` | normal |
| `warning <= upper < alarm` | warning |
| `upper >= alarm` | alarm |

Equality at the alarm threshold is therefore alarm. An uncertainty interval
that merely reaches alarm is also alarm; the nominal value does not have to
cross it.

The Digital Temperature mapping is fixed and categorical: `Below` means
normal, while `AtOrAbove` means alarm. A categorical alarm makes the combined
thermal result `Alarm`. A categorical `Below` paired with a thermistor warning
or alarm reports `Disagreement`. If the thermistor interval reaches alarm,
`thermalHazard` remains true during disagreement, so contrary evidence cannot
suppress the possible hazard.

Stale, saturated, or producer-fault evidence never participates in agreement
voting. Invalid evidence cannot become `Normal`.

equality at the sustained threshold, continued sustained activity, and an
inactive edge; a second strip shows a short completed pulse and a longer
completed candidate](../downloads/assets/062-radiant-timeline-pencil.png)

## Radiant pulse and sustained state

The radiant path treats `AtOrAbove` as a unitless active threshold state. It
does not assign a physical cause.

- An inactive-to-active edge starts one candidate at the copied observation
  time.
- While active, elapsed time below `radiantSustainedMinimum` is
  `AbruptChange`; equality and later are `Sustained`.
- An active-to-inactive edge whose total duration is at most
  `radiantPulseMaximum` remains `AbruptChange`.
- A longer completed candidate is conservatively `Sustained`.
- Saturated ambient evidence reports `SaturatedAmbient` and clears the
  candidate.

At equal sequence and observation time, only a byte-identical copy of the
complete envelope is an accepted duplicate; any changed field rejects
atomically. Replaying that full duplicate at a valid later policy time is
idempotent: it does not advance the last-update time, age the candidate, or
extend its duration. Source or revision changes, backward time, exact-half-range
ambiguity, and sequence regression reject atomically. After a role reaches
`UINT32_MAX`, that role may remain byte-identical while another role advances;
attempting to change the exhausted role rejects with `CapacityExceeded`.
The last accepted observation and candidate history remain unchanged.

![a pencil matrix compares thermistor normal, warning, and alarm rows with
Digital Temperature below and at-or-above columns; disagreement cells
preserve the thermistor hazard flag instead of voting it away](../downloads/assets/062-thermal-agreement-pencil.png)

## Status and quality precedence

`Status` reports invalid configuration, lifecycle misuse, malformed identity
or ordering, and producer failure propagation. `ThermalQuality` and
`RadiantQuality` report accepted domain outcomes.

The policy validates the complete three-source envelope before committing any
state. Producer fault, saturation, freshness, thermal agreement, and radiant
duration are then evaluated without allowing one role to erase another role's
evidence. A rejected update is atomic: it cannot partially advance one source
sequence, one age, or the radiant candidate.

If several copied producers report a non-OK `Status` together, the deterministic
status precedence is thermistor, then Digital Temperature, then radiant. This
ordering selects the returned status only. The complete copied envelope and
side-specific quality retain the other producer-fault evidence; precedence
does not validate a producer or erase a collision.

## Deterministic fixtures

Host fixtures use supplied time and copied values only:

| Fixture | Boundary | Expected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| uncertainty below warning | upper bound one unit below warning | `Normal`, no thermal hazard |
| warning equality | upper bound exactly warning | `Warning` |
| alarm equality | upper bound exactly alarm | `Alarm`, thermal hazard |
| categorical alarm | Digital Temperature `AtOrAbove` | combined `Alarm` without inventing units |
| contrary categorical source | thermistor warning/alarm with Digital Temperature `Below` | `Disagreement`; a possible thermistor alarm remains hazardous |
| short radiant candidate | active duration at pulse maximum | `AbruptChange` |
| sustained equality | active duration exactly sustained minimum | `Sustained`, radiant hazard |
| saturated radiant evidence | radiant saturation flag set | `SaturatedAmbient`; candidate cleared |
| independent age edge | each source at maximum age, then one unit older | accepted edge, then side-specific stale evidence |
| duplicate/regression | replay identical evidence, alter a duplicate, then regress | idempotence followed by atomic rejection |

Tests additionally cover signed temperature extremes without overflow, every
warning/alarm neighbor, all thermal/categorical agreement combinations,
radiant rise/fall and pulse/sustained neighbors, source collisions, producer
faults, future samples, modular rollover, exact-half-range ambiguity, reset,
sequence exhaustion, and byte-identical full replay.

## Compile-only Mega replay

The canonical Mega 2560 sketch is an inert replay. It supplies fixed synthetic
envelopes and explicit times, then copies each result into named volatile
cells. Those cells are the non-Serial E0 observation path:

| Stage | Predict | Observe | Interpret |
|---:|---|---|---|
| initialize | no source qualified | lifecycle and `Unqualified` cells | policy is ready; no resource was acquired |
| normal envelope | three independent fresh roles | copied identities, ages, and both quality cells | the roles remain attributable and noninterchangeable |
| uncertainty crossing | thermistor interval reaches alarm | interval, `Alarm`, and thermal-hazard cells | conservative arithmetic retained the possible hazard |
| disagreement | Digital Temperature remains `Below` | both side states, `Disagreement`, and hazard cells | categorical contradiction did not erase thermistor evidence |
| radiant pulse | short active candidate returns inactive | candidate duration and `AbruptChange` cells | a copied edge sequence was classified, not physically stimulated |
| radiant sustained | active evidence reaches the exact boundary | duration, `Sustained`, and radiant-hazard cells | supplied-time equality follows the fixed rule |
| saturation/stale | one role is saturated or too old | side-specific quality and provenance cells | unhealthy evidence did not vote as normal |
| reset | ordering and candidate history clear | unqualified snapshot and fresh-sequence result cells | volatile policy state cleared; no physical safe-state claim follows |

Predict the named cells before each update and compare them with the
deterministic host trace. Mega compilation is packaging evidence only. Do not
upload or run this E0 sketch on a powered Mega, connect any sensor, or infer a
physical safe state from memory cells.

## Resource and safe-state evidence

The canonical Mega replay measures 10,076 bytes of flash and 1,133 bytes of
static SRAM. The ordinary static-SRAM result is an independently accepted
target miss against 1,024 bytes and remains below the 1,536-byte hard limit.
The exact isolated no-LTO gate measures 5,156 bytes of flash, 279 bytes of
static SRAM, 264 bytes of conservative synchronous stack, and a 112-byte
policy object. Its caller retains 59 bytes of stack and a 76-byte observation,
for 135 caller-owned bytes, leaving 7,521 bytes of residual museum-monitor
SRAM. The fingerprinted compiler evidence and public-value checks pass.
These are compiler measurements, not runtime, electrical, optical, thermal,
or physical evidence.

At E0, resource-acquisition evidence is exactly “no resources requested.”
Separate safe-state evidence is the reset/unqualified memory snapshot. Neither
statement proves that a thermistor divider or module is unpowered, that a
radiant source is inactive, or that any physical circuit is safe.

## E1b remains open

No wiring table or formal schematic is authorized at E0. Each exact
thermistor, distinct Digital Temperature module, and radiant module must be
qualified independently before its adapter, units, polarity, or thresholds
are published. E1b evidence must cover:

- exact markings, PCB revision, pin order, primary sources, supply limits, and
  loaded output rails;
- thermistor divider topology, ADC reference, proved fixed-point curve,
  calibration range, uncertainty, self-heating, and settling;
- the Digital Temperature source's actual transport or threshold topology
  without assuming 18B20 identity;
- radiant response and threshold polarity, ambient saturation, target/source
  geometry, current, and independent non-Serial observation;
- transactional acquisition, rollback, shutdown, powered-off behavior, and
  separate resource-acquisition and safe-state records; and
- attended bench acceptance for each source before any combined monitor test.

Any later radiant stimulus must be an owned, documented harmless low-energy IR
target/source pair or an owned TV remote used only as a stimulus. Exposure is
limited to at most five seconds followed by at least thirty seconds inactive.
It is never replayed, aimed at eyes, used continuously or unattended, or used
with an unknown target. Flame, heaters, hot wires, combustible targets,
ignition, pyrotechnics, and lasers remain excluded.

Lesson 062 makes no preservation, calibrated-radiant, fire-detection,
life-safety, physical-temperature, transport, or powered-module claim.
