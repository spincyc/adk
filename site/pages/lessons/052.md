# Lesson 052 — copied infrared capture evidence

Status: **E0 synthetic receive-evidence integration; exact receiver, powered
indicators, and bench acceptance open**

Energy class: **E0 copied-evidence policy**
Safety boundary: **borrowed published frames and caller-owned word storage
only; no receiver, carrier, emitter, or optical power path**

`CapturedIrEvidence` turns one briefly borrowed Lesson 025 `PulseFrame` into
an attributable, stable record:

`published pulse frame + decoder + source identity + observation time → copied receive evidence`

The result may describe a known command, a repeat shape, an unknown protocol,
malformed timing or integrity, capture overflow, or a source fault. It is
evidence about reception only. It is never a transmission request, learned
remote entry, permission to replay an unknown waveform, or claim that
hardware received anything.

## The IR detective story

The canonical compile-only Mega sketch stages a small investigation using
synthetic published frames.

1. **Acquire:** `setup()` constructs an `InfraredDecoder`, reserves a
   caller-owned word array, defines one synthetic source identity, and
   initializes `CapturedIrEvidence`.
2. **Observe:** `loop()` presents one checked-in `PulseFrame` and supplies the
   time at which software examines it.
3. **Decide:** the component decodes synchronously, checks the capture and
   decoder sequence, classifies the result, and copies every admitted pulse
   before the borrowed frame could be acknowledged.
4. **Actuate:** the sketch writes only named memory result cells for known,
   repeat, unknown, malformed, and fault outcomes.

The replay expands that flow across five frames:

| Stage | Predict |
|---:|---|
| 1 | a complete valid NEC-shaped frame becomes `KnownValid` with `IntegrityVerified` strength |
| 2 | the short NEC repeat shape becomes `KnownRepeat` with `ShapeRecognized` strength and no inherited command |
| 3 | an unsupported shape becomes `UnknownProtocol` with `None` strength |
| 4 | a ten-pulse NEC prefix becomes `Truncated` with `ShapeRecognized` strength |
| 5 | a complete trace carrying producer `HardwareFailure` becomes attributable `SourceFault` evidence |

Each pass records capture sequence, evidence generation, decoded fields,
first and last copied words, disposition, strength, pulse count, operation
status, evidence status, view status, and a prediction result. Predict those
cells before each stage. Then inspect them in executable host tests and
interpret them as proof of deterministic software classification—not proof
of a receiver, remote, LED, or powered Arduino run.

## Stable copy, explicit ownership

Lesson 025 still owns pulse capture, publication lifetime, and
acknowledgement. Lesson 052 does not alter `PulseCapture`, acknowledge a
frame, rearm capture, retain a pointer into capture storage, or own another
interrupt path.

The caller must invoke `admit()` while the Lesson 025 frame is still
published. `CapturedIrEvidence` asks its borrowed `InfraredDecoder` to decode
that same frame, verifies that both sequences agree, and copies each pulse
into the supplied `IrPulseStorage`. Only after successful admission may the
caller choose whether to acknowledge the original frame.

One `uint32_t` word stores each copied pulse. Bit 31 distinguishes `Mark` from
`Space`; bits 0–30 hold a nonzero microsecond duration. The fixed upper bound
is the Lesson 025 capacity of 100 pulses. This is an in-memory public value
representation, not a byte serialization or persistence format.

Construction is inert. The storage pointer must remain valid and unmoved
while the component is initialized. `reset()`, `shutdown()`, destruction, or
a successful new non-duplicate admission invalidates an earlier
`CapturedIrView`. Foreign and stale owner/generation views reject. A short
export destination is detected before writing and remains unchanged.

## Provenance before interpretation

Every accepted record retains:

- source kind, nonzero source ID, configuration revision, and session epoch;
- the complete producer `Status`;
- the Lesson 025 capture sequence and capture state;
- the supplied `observedAt` time;
- decoder protocol and validity;
- categorical disposition and evidence strength;
- decoded address and command only for known-valid evidence; and
- pulse count, copied words, and a nonzero evidence generation.

`observedAt` means when software copied the published frame. Lesson 025 does
not publish capture-start, capture-complete, optical-arrival, or occurrence
time, so Lesson 052 does not invent one.

An exact same-sequence duplicate is idempotent: it retains the first
observation time and evidence generation. Changed content at the same
sequence is a source fault. Forward order, wrap, regression, and the
half-range ambiguity follow the repository's unsigned modular ordering rule.
Changing source, configuration, or session requires `reset()`.

## Disposition is not confidence

`EvidenceStrength` is categorical:

| Strength | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `None` | no supported protocol shape establishes additional structure |
| `ShapeRecognized` | the decoder recognized limited NEC shape, but not a new valid command |
| `IntegrityVerified` | a known-valid frame passed the decoder's integrity checks |

This is not a probability, confidence score, authenticity result, or
authorization. A repeat is separately reported and never inherits an earlier
address or command. Unknown, malformed, overflow, and repeat records keep
canonical zero decoded fields. Their copied pulse words remain available for
receive-side inspection, but no Lesson 052 API can turn them into emission
intent.

The exact `IrCaptureDisposition` preserves whether evidence was known valid,
known repeat, unknown protocol, timing-invalid, integrity-invalid, truncated,
decoder-overflow, capture-overflow, capture-timing-fault, or source-fault.
Impossible capture/decoder combinations reject atomically instead of being
normalized into a plausible story.

## Deterministic proof

Host tests exercise null and bounded storage, every pulse-count and duration
edge, every disposition, prohibited state combinations, sequence mismatch,
complete provenance, duplicate and changed replay, wrap and half-range
ordering, copy atomicity, reset and shutdown, stale and foreign views, and
short/exact/large exports. Tests compare fields and copied words rather than
struct padding.

The E0 boundary claims no pins, timers, interrupts, buses, endpoints,
receivers, emitters, supplies, or current paths. The canonical Mega build
measures **5,530 bytes of flash** and **1,096 bytes of static SRAM**.
`CapturedIrEvidence` itself measures **48 bytes on AVR**, excluding the
caller-owned pulse-word array. Conservative inspection estimates the
sketch's live stack at approximately **197 bytes**.

These are compiler, linker, object-layout, and conservative static-analysis
results. The stack figure is not a measured runtime high-water mark. None of
the figures is bench evidence, proof that a board ran the fixture, or proof
that infrared light was observed.

E1 remains open until an exact receiver, owned harmless remote, supply range,
pin order, idle polarity, output level, carrier band, Mega input
compatibility, interrupt allocation, powered observation path, authoritative
formal schematic, and bench record are qualified. Resource acquisition and
rollback, pulse observation, visible classification, shutdown pin state,
reset, saturation, missing-carrier behavior, and power removal require
separate physical evidence.

## Use with

Use Lesson 052 with:

- [Lesson 025 — infrared evidence](025.md), which owns capture, publication,
  decoding foundations, and acknowledgement;
- Lesson 053, which introduces an independent immutable catalog of
  learner-authored local commands and bounded inert emission intent; and
- Lesson 054, which may map one valid attributable receive symbol to a
  different fixed local symbol.

Do not feed `PulseFrame`, `InfraredFrame`, `CapturedIrView`, decoded raw
address/command fields, copied durations, or arbitrary bytes into Lesson 053.
Transmission authority comes only from its firmware-authored local catalog.
Lesson 054 cannot learn mappings, infer authority from repeats, use identity
mappings, bridge raw protocols, or replay unknown evidence.
