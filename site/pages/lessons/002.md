# Lesson 002: two independent LEDs

Build two current-limited LED branches on pins D4 and D7 and show that a shared
ground does not prevent independent control.

## Outcomes

By the end, you can:

- trace a complete output-to-ground current path;
- identify LED anode and cathode;
- calculate a conservative series resistor and estimated branch current;
- command two `adk::led::Mono` objects independently;
- diagnose a reversed LED or misplaced breadboard connection; and
- collect repeated evidence for independent control.

## Prerequisites

- [Lesson 001](001.md)
- Ability to identify Mega 2560 pin labels and breadboard-connected rows
- Basic voltage, resistance, and Ohm's-law vocabulary

## Bill of materials

- Arduino Mega 2560 and USB data cable
- Breadboard
- Two through-hole LEDs, preferably visibly different colors
- Two 330 Ω resistors, one for each LED
- Jumper wires
- Multimeter with a DC-voltage range

Current measurement is optional and should be attempted only with an
instructor-approved series connection.

## Safety

Disconnect every power source before inserting, moving, or removing a
component. Never connect an LED directly from an output to ground, never share
one resistor between these branches, and never connect D4 directly to D7.
Do not place a meter in current mode across a pin and ground. Remove power
immediately for heat, unusual brightness, resets, smoke, or odor.

## Current API and wiring

```cpp
adk::led::Mono red  (4);
adk::led::Mono blue (7);

adk::initialize ();
red.on          ();
blue.off        ();
```

Build two separate series branches:

- D4 → 330 Ω resistor → red LED anode; red cathode → GND
- D7 → 330 Ω resistor → blue LED anode; blue cathode → GND

Lead order can vary, especially after leads have been trimmed. Verify the
actual part rather than relying only on lead length.
