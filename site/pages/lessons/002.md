# Digital input

Status: **host verified; Mega hardware acceptance pending**

Energy class: **E1** — one USB-powered Mega 2560, one identified dry-contact
switch, the board's built-in LED, DMM, and measured 10 kΩ D13 weak-bias
resistor. No external supply is admitted.

Wire a normally-open button between D22 and GND. `Pull::Up` keeps the released
input high; a press reads low. `DigitalInput` reports electrical level only:
Button meaning and debounce arrive in lesson 003.

```cpp
adk::Runtime      runtime;
adk::DigitalInput button (runtime.resources (), 22, adk::Pull::Up);

button.initialize ();
button.update     ();
const adk::Level level = button.read ();
```

`read()` returns the cached update snapshot. `sample()` performs an immediate
diagnostic read. The canonical sketch emits no Serial log: the built-in LED is
the primary observation path, with D22 (`TP-IN`) measured relative to GND when
an electrical diagnostic is needed.

Before power, record the board maker/model/revision and marking, USB source
identity and 5 V rating, and cable identity. Identify the switch by part or kit
label, and use continuity mode to identify its actual open/closed contact pair.
Predict released = D22 HIGH/LED off and pressed = D22 LOW/LED on; observe both;
interpret agreement against the pull-up and GND paths.

Successful acquisition produces a 250 ms LED pulse and 750 ms dark separator
before switch behavior begins. Hold the switch continuously for three seconds
to exercise the controlled
shutdown path. The sketch releases D22 and `LED_BUILTIN` to `INPUT` and stays
passive until reset. Host tests establish the pin-mode trace. Physical D22
evidence uses a named DMM, connected TP-IN-to-GND before power, whose input
resistance is recorded. Calculate the released voltage with the Mega pull-up
active and the post-shutdown voltage with only the meter's weak path; record
both rather than inferring high impedance from LED darkness. Reset must
reacquire both pins and restore the mapping. Software shutdown does **not**
remove power:
disconnect USB and every other source before touching wiring.

D13 uses the same physical method as Lesson 001: with USB removed, install an
identified, measured 10 kΩ weak pull-up from D13 to the measured 5 V rail and
record the exact board LED circuit. The sketch commands D13 LOW for 250 ms
before releasing it; record the driven-LOW and later weak-bias voltages against
calculated board-specific ranges.

The PDF contains the exact schematic, identity record, truth table,
predict-observe-interpret matrix, bounce observations, fault lab, reset and
power-removal checks, and bench acceptance card.
