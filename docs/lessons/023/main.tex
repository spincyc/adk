\begin{center}
{\Huge\bfseries The three-lamp interlock}\\[4pt]
{\Large Lesson 023 --- ask, admit, light exactly one}\\[6pt]
\textit{A deliberate failure makes every load lamp go dark; then the circuit recovers.}
\end{center}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}lX>{\bfseries}lX@{}}
Time & about 35 minutes & Board & Arduino Mega 2560\\
Payoffs & chase, fail-closed scene, recovery & Energy & E1, USB only\\
Physical parts & four LEDs, three 220 \(\Omega\), one 1 k\(\Omega\) & Loads & names only\\
\end{tabularx}

This is a three-lamp policy machine. ``Fan,'' ``pump,'' and ``heater'' are
names for three ordinary LEDs. The sketch never connects or controls a relay,
driver, motor, pump, heater, external supply, or mains circuit. D13 blinks
before each request; D40, D38, and D41 show the one admitted choice; D12 shows
the deliberate fault.

\textbf{Hard boundary.} Keep every relay module, transistor board, motor,
external supply, and energetic load off the table. An LED demonstrates
software intent, not isolation or safe load power. Unplug USB before moving a
wire. Stop for heat, smell, smoke, repeated USB resets, two load lamps glowing
together, or any disagreement between the plate and the circuit.

\section*{Adventure map}

\begin{center}
% ADK visual: pencil
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{023-progress-pencil.png}
\end{center}
\textbf{Plate 1: four dependent visible wins.} First the three applied lamps
chase. Next a rejected fan request makes all three dark while D12 stays on for
two seconds. Explicit shutdown and initialization clear that sticky software
fault, so the chase succeeds again. At 15 seconds every lamp goes dark, stays
inactive for 250 ms, releases its pin, and waits for Reset. No input can extend
that deadline.

\section*{Stage 1: build one literal breadboard}

Gather a Mega 2560, USB cable, breadboard, four ordinary LEDs, three
220 \(\Omega\) resistors, one 1 k\(\Omega\) resistor, and jumpers. Identify
each LED's anode and cathode from the actual part; do not infer polarity from
color. The built-in D13 LED needs no wire.

\textbf{Plate 2: actual header pairs, breadboard rows, and component legs.}
The paired Mega header puts even D38 and D40 in one physical row and odd D41
beside D40. D12 is on the top digital header. Breadboard holes a--e in one
numbered row are connected; f--j form a separate group across the center gap.
The two negative rails are joined at the far end and return to one Mega GND.

Build the pump branch first. Compare a5, e8, e10, and the negative rail with
Plate 2. Add fan, heater, then fault one branch at a time. This order keeps a
mistake local and makes the finished layout easy to compare hole by hole.

\section*{Stage 2: watch mutual exclusion}

Restore USB and upload the canonical
\texttt{Lesson023InertLoadInterlock} sketch. Within a third of a second D13
blinks and the fan lamp on D40 lights. The visible sequence is:

\begin{center}
D40 fan \(\rightarrow\) D38 pump \(\rightarrow\) D41 heater
\(\rightarrow\) all off.
\end{center}

Each change begins with a short D13 pulse. One applied lamp turns off before
the next turns on. If a lamp is missing, unplug and inspect only its named pin,
resistor holes, LED legs, and ground return. If two glow together, unplug
immediately and compare every occupied hole with Plate 2.

This win depends on the complete path:
\[
\text{request pulse}\rightarrow\text{selection}\rightarrow
\text{admission}\rightarrow\text{panel}\rightarrow\text{one LED}.
\]
Serial output is not needed.

\section*{Stage 3: enjoy the fail-closed scene}

After the first all-off moment, the sketch deliberately revokes its
\emph{software} command admission and asks for fan again. D13 still blinks,
but D40, D38, and D41 remain dark. D12 lights for about two seconds.

That disagreement is the point: a request happened, policy refused to apply
it, and a distinct lamp made the fault visible. The scene supports a
fail-closed software decision in this inert circuit. It does not prove
galvanic isolation, relay-contact position, stored-energy removal, or safe
control of a real load.

\section*{Stage 4: recovery earns the second chase}

Restoring admission alone cannot erase the sticky fault. The sketch explicitly
shuts down the panel and interlock, restores admission, initializes both, and
clears D12. Only then does the fan--pump--heater chase run a second time.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=adkcpp]
panel.shutdown ();
interlock.shutdown ();
admission.admit ();

if (interlock.initialize ().ok () &&
    panel.initialize ().ok ())
{
    faultEvidence.off ();
}
\end{lstlisting}

The second chase is therefore a dependent reward: it appears only if
acquisition, the first chase, deliberate rejection, all-off application,
sticky-fault handling, and explicit lifecycle recovery all worked.

\section*{Stage 5: discover the fixed ending}

At 15 seconds, regardless of the active stage, the sketch revokes admission,
selects every simulated output inactive, turns D13 and D12 off, holds the
observable inactive state for 250 ms, releases the indicator resources, and
waits. Press Reset to play again.

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.28\linewidth}X@{}}
\toprule
What you see & What it supports\\\midrule
D13 then exactly one load lamp & a request preceded one admitted inert intent\\
D13, three dark load lamps, D12 on & a recorded request failed closed visibly\\
D12 clears, chase repeats & explicit lifecycle recovery completed\\
all lamps dark at 15 seconds & fixed inactive hold and shutdown began\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Darkness is circuit-native evidence. Building, uploading, and watching this
story still does not claim separately recorded physical verification.

\section*{Under the hood}

\texttt{InertLoadInterlock} owns no pin. It records requested and active
intent separately and admits at most one active value.
\texttt{InertLoadPanel} borrows three \texttt{IndicatorPump} objects and
renders the active value break-before-make. Construction is inert;
\texttt{initialize()} acquires resources; \texttt{shutdown()} is idempotent
and selects inactive before release.

The automated repository suite stays behind the scenes. It exhausts all
selection transitions, invalid values, endpoint acquisition failures,
old-off and new-on failures, admission revocation, sticky faults, explicit
recovery, repeated shutdown, destruction, and deterministic replay. Its core
trace property is
\[
[\mathrm{fan}(t)]+[\mathrm{pump}(t)]+[\mathrm{heater}(t)]\leq1.
\]
Those tests protect the lesson without turning the learner's visible adventure
into a test procedure.

\section*{Make it yours}

Change one harmless presentation detail at a time: reorder the three simulated
names, make D13 pulse twice before the fault scene, shorten the ordinary chase
interval, or place paper fan/pump/heater labels beside the LEDs. Keep the
circuit USB-powered and resistor-limited. Never substitute a real load.
