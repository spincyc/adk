\lessonbox{\textbf{Scope.} This lesson exercises the current pure
\texttt{I2cBus}, \texttt{I2cDevice}, \texttt{SpiBus}, and
\texttt{SpiDevice} interfaces with sketch-local recording drivers. Every
transfer receives a bounded timeout value. The lesson generates no SDA, SCL,
MOSI, MISO, or SCK waveform and communicates with no physical device.
\texttt{MegaI2cDriver}, \texttt{MegaSpiDriver}, and their direct AVR backends
now own the fixed pins and restore chip select in deterministic tests. This
recording-driver sketch deliberately keeps their physical bench evidence open.}

\section{Mission}

Build a repeatable transaction story:
\[
\text{TP-M}\rightarrow\texttt{MoistureSample}\rightarrow
\text{bounded record}\rightarrow\text{visible acknowledgement}.
\]
Turn a 10 k\(\Omega\) potentiometer to create a controlled analog voltage.
\texttt{MoistureSensor} maps the configured dry and wet readings to
0--1000 permille while keeping validity separate from the number.
\texttt{SimulatedRtc} supplies explicit clock state, a synthetic I2C device
exercises owned addressing, and a SPI recording transaction carries the same
11-byte record into \texttt{FixedStorage}. Only a successful \texttt{sync()}
advances its durable prefix.

The exercise separates five ideas that are often hidden behind global
\texttt{Wire} and \texttt{SPI} objects:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a physical observation and its interpreted sample are distinct;
  \item a bus owner has one explicit lifetime;
  \item a transaction has bounded, caller-owned buffers;
  \item a driver performs transport but does not decide application policy;
  \item an observation must say exactly what it proves.
\end{enumerate}

D13 pulses after a valid moisture sample receives a valid clock reading,
passes both recording drivers, appends, and synchronizes. Its duration ranges
from 100 ms at 0 permille to 900 ms at 1000 permille. D12 latches on an
invalid sample, invalid clock state, transport failure, append failure, sync
failure, or capacity exhaustion. The pulse proves the deterministic software
path and in-memory durability model. TP-M is the independent electrical
observation; neither proves a physical bus transaction or durable SD media.

\section{From voltage to a valid sample}

The reference calibration is:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=adkcpp]
const adk::MoistureCalibration moistureCalibration = {200, 800, 32};
\end{lstlisting}

A raw reading of 200 represents the chosen dry endpoint and maps to
0 permille. A raw reading of 800 represents the chosen wet endpoint and maps
to 1000 permille. Values within 32 counts beyond either endpoint are clamped;
larger excursions become \texttt{InputBelowRange} or
\texttt{InputAboveRange}. The names describe electrical range direction, not
a diagnosis of soil.

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l X@{}}
\toprule
State & Numeric field & Meaning\\
\midrule
\texttt{Unavailable} & not usable & no accepted update or sensor shut down\\
\texttt{Valid} & 0--1000 permille & calibrated reading accepted\\
\texttt{InputBelowRange} & zero & reading below the guarded electrical range\\
\texttt{InputAboveRange} & zero & reading above the guarded electrical range\\
\texttt{Stale} & retained value & last valid observation is older than policy\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The potentiometer is a repeatable voltage source, not a soil model. ADC counts
are ratios to the measured reference rail. Record TP-M relative to GND and do
not call an unmeasured count a voltage. A future soil probe needs exact module
identity, supply and output limits, source impedance, common ground, corrosion
policy, and a separate bench card.

\subsection{Integer mapping}

\[
m=\operatorname{round}\left(
\frac{(\mathrm{raw}-\mathrm{dry})1000}{\mathrm{wet}-\mathrm{dry}}
\right).
\]

The implementation uses signed comparisons around the guarded endpoints,
integer-only nearest rounding, and clamping after validity checks. Ascending
and descending calibration both work. Calling \texttt{sample(now, staleAfter)}
does not mutate the stored result; repeated observation is idempotent.

Construction is inert. \texttt{initialize()} claims the named bus resource,
then configures its driver. If configuration fails, the owner disables the
driver and releases the claim. Repeated initialization is idempotent.
\texttt{shutdown()} disables transport, releases the claim, and may be called
again. Destruction calls shutdown, making cleanup safe during a caller's
exception-driven unwinding even though ADK throws no exceptions.

\subsection{Why the driver is injected}

The same owner can use a deterministic host fake, this lesson's recording
driver, or the direct Mega adapter. The owner controls lifetime and request
validation. The driver controls platform effects. Neither owns application
meaning such as ``read a clock'' or ``append a record.''

The recording drivers deliberately return computed bytes. That makes repeated
runs observable to code without pretending the bytes came from hardware.

\section{Request boundaries}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\toprule
Owner & Current validation\\
\midrule
\texttt{I2cBus} & initialized; address 0x08--0x77; at least one direction;
  each buffer at most 32 bytes; nonzero sizes require non-null buffers\\
\texttt{SpiBus} & initialized; nonzero clock; mode 0--3; 1--256 bytes;
  at least one buffer; chip-select pin must be claimable\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The caller retains every buffer and may reuse or discard it after
\texttt{transfer()} returns. The bus stores no callback and starts no hidden
queue. Every call includes a nonzero \texttt{Duration} timeout. A physical
driver must honor that bound: ``synchronous'' must not mean ``wait forever.''

\subsection{Status is policy input}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=adkcpp]
const adk::Status status =
    metadataDevice.transfer (writeData, writeSize, readData, readSize,
                             adk::Duration (20));

if (!status.ok ())
{
    observeFailure (status);
}
\end{lstlisting}

Pass the complete \texttt{Status} to the observation boundary. Use
\texttt{error()} only when a named policy needs the underlying
\texttt{StatusCode}. \texttt{transient()} classifies a potentially recoverable
cause; it is not permission to retry blindly. A retry policy belongs above the
bus and must be bounded and observable.

\section{Clock state and durability}

\texttt{Rtc::read()} returns \texttt{Result<ClockReading>}. Transport status
and clock state answer different questions. A successful transport may still
report \texttt{NotSet} or \texttt{OscillatorStopped}; neither is acceptable as
a trustworthy record timestamp.

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\toprule
Clock state & Record policy\\
\midrule
\texttt{Valid} & serialize raw Unix seconds; civil-time formatting stays out\\
\texttt{NotSet} & reject timestamp and show fault\\
\texttt{OscillatorStopped} & reject timestamp and preserve diagnostic state\\
\texttt{TransportFault} & propagate complete failure status\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The sketch uses a clearly named \texttt{SimulatedRtc}: it starts at a fixed
Unix-seconds value and advances deterministically on reads. It is not an I2C
RTC adapter and survives no power interruption.

\texttt{Storage::append()} stages bytes. It does not claim durability.
\texttt{Storage::sync()} is the commit boundary. \texttt{FixedStorageMedium}
holds a bounded 512-byte in-memory medium while \texttt{FixedStorage} owns one
session. Shutting down and reinitializing the owner discards any unsynchronized
suffix and resumes at the exact durable prefix. A failed sync leaves the staged
suffix available for a bounded retry without re-appending; restarting instead
discards it.

The record layout is fixed and endian-explicit:

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l X@{}}
\toprule
Bytes & Field & Meaning\\
\midrule
0--3 & Unix seconds & unsigned, most-significant byte first\\
4 & clock state & explicit \texttt{ClockState}\\
5--6 & raw ADC & unsigned, most-significant byte first\\
7--8 & moisture & permille, most-significant byte first\\
9 & sample state & explicit \texttt{MoistureSampleState}\\
10 & metadata token & deterministic I2C recording-driver response\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Every eighth committed record, the sketch restarts the \texttt{FixedStorage}
owner and compares its staged size with the medium's durable size. This proves
session restart against the in-memory model, not board reset or power-loss
retention. At fixed capacity, the next append fails visibly rather than
overwriting an earlier record.

\section{Narrative sketch}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=adkcpp]
void loop ()
{
    const adk::TimePoint now (millis ());

    clearAcknowledgement (now);

    if (sampleIsDue (now))
    {
        adk::MoistureSample sample;

        observeMoisture     (now, sample);
        persistMoisture     (now, sample);
        acknowledgeMoisture (now, sample);
    }
}
\end{lstlisting}

The canonical example names the high-level actions before recording mechanics.
Setup acquires the evidence LEDs, bus and device owners, moisture sensor,
simulated RTC, and storage session. Each scheduled cycle observes TP-M, rejects
a non-valid sample or clock, obtains one deterministic I2C metadata token,
sends the 11-byte record through the SPI recording driver, appends and
synchronizes the same bytes, then pulses D13.

The implementation uses elapsed unsigned time and no blocking
\texttt{delay()}. A recorded input trace produces the same samples, records,
statuses, and pulse durations on every replay.

\section{Experiment A: calibrate TP-M}

\textbf{Predict.} At raw 200, the sample is valid at 0 permille and D13 pulses
for about 100 ms. At raw 500, it is near 500 permille and the pulse is near
500 ms. At raw 800, it is 1000 permille and the pulse is near 900 ms.

\textbf{Observe.} With USB removed, place meter clips between TP-M and GND.
Restore USB, turn the shaft, and record raw count, TP-M, sample state,
permille, and D13 pulse duration. Serial may reveal the count during a
diagnostic continuation but is not required to see TP-M or the pulse.

\textbf{Interpret.} Increasing pulse duration supports the calibrated mapping.
TP-M supports the electrical input. D13 supports accepted interpretation and
record-path completion. It does not prove soil moisture.

\section{Experiment B: predict ownership}

\textbf{Predict.} With D12 already claimed by another component,
\texttt{faultEvidence.initialize()} fails before the two bus owners are
started. With all resources free, both owners initialize.

\textbf{Observe.} Use the deterministic host tests for claim conflicts. On the
Mega example, observe whether the normal transaction acknowledgement begins.

\textbf{Interpret.} A missing pulse can reject the hypothesis that the whole
path completed. A pulse supports the software path only. The landed Mega
drivers own D20/D21 for I2C and D50--D53 for SPI, but this recording-driver
experiment does not exercise those pins.

\section{Experiment C: inspect a transaction}

\textbf{Predict.} Every successful cycle records I2C address 0x50, one written
byte, and one synthetic metadata token. SPI then records mode 0, 1 MHz,
most-significant-bit first, D49 as the logical chip-select identifier, and 11
bytes. Append advances only the staged size; sync advances the durable size.

\textbf{Observe.} Run the host test and inspect the recording driver's exact
fields. Run it twice from a clean fixture.

\textbf{Interpret.} Equal records support deterministic request construction.
They do not establish I2C acknowledgement, SPI edge timing, voltage
compatibility, or chip-select restoration on a Mega.

\section{Experiment D: distinguish two proofs}

\textbf{Resource proof.} A successful first transaction supports that both bus
owners and persistent D49 device claim were available to this software path.

\textbf{Safe-state proof.} Host tests show the landed Mega adapters restore
their bus pins and chip select to documented inactive states after shutdown
and injected failure. The recording driver has no physical pins, so this lesson
cannot turn that trace into a measurement. D12 and D13 can demonstrate their
own LED endpoint cleanup only.

\section{Fault laboratory}

Work through these cases with recording fakes before attaching a peripheral:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item reject I2C reserved addresses 0x00 and 0x78;
  \item reject null data with a nonzero size;
  \item accept the exact maximum sizes and reject one byte beyond;
  \item inject driver-configuration failure and prove claim rollback;
  \item inject transfer failure and preserve its complete \texttt{Status};
  \item conflict on D49 and prove the SPI driver is not called;
  \item inject append failure at every byte and retain only the exact prefix;
  \item fail sync, restart the owner, and discard the unsynchronized suffix;
  \item fill the fixed capacity and reject the next record without overwrite;
  \item replay every \texttt{ClockState} separately from transport status;
  \item call shutdown twice and then reuse each released resource;
  \item destroy initialized owners and prove the same reuse.
\end{enumerate}

\lessonbox{\textbf{Design question.} \texttt{SpiDevice} owns D49 and asks its
driver to configure and release that chip select. What recording operations
must the Mega driver expose so tests prove ``assert, transfer, restore'' across
success, partial failure, shutdown, and destruction?}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Reverse the dry and wet calibration endpoints. Predict the three
  sample values and pulse durations before replaying them.
  \item Draw the owner, driver, device, and application-policy lifetimes. Mark
  every borrowed relationship.
  \item Add a fake-driver failure at the first byte and state which facts may
  still be claimed.
  \item Propose a timeout value object without reading a global clock inside
  the pure bus owner.
  \item Design a non-owning I2C device relationship that cannot outlive its
  owner. Compare generation tokens, scoped transactions, and explicit
  registration.
  \item Write the SPI restoration invariant for success, invalid input,
  resource conflict, partial transport failure, shutdown, and destruction.
\end{enumerate}
