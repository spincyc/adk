# Resolve Lesson 030 composition blockers

Independent API, lifecycle, replay, safety, timing, and test audits agreed that
the earlier design could not be implemented without inferring cue IDs as
channel IDs, synthesizing hidden operator input, or contradicting dependency
ownership. Those alternatives violate the project boundary.

Lesson 030 will instead copy an explicit plan-index-to-channel map and validate
it against a narrow read-only scheduler plan view. Lesson 029 remains
independent of Lesson 028. The scheduler will gain a channel-agnostic
Permit/Hold/Fault evidence gate and remain the sole cue-audit writer.
Same-timestamp full-frame identity will be checked before event precedence.

Dependencies are constructed but initially uninitialized. The simulator
coordinates assessor then scheduler initialization with reverse rollback;
scheduler shutdown precedes assessor shutdown and leaves the audit readable.
Complete frames contain all eight unique channels and are canonicalized by
channel. Open, short-simulated, stale, and unavailable are nonterminal hold
conditions; contradiction, invalid input, audit exhaustion, and invariant
failure are faults. The total new-frame precedence is cancel, invalid chord,
evidence fault/hold, review release, phase action, then timing.

This is the smallest value-only correction that preserves deterministic
composition without exposing pins, callbacks, protocols, generic outputs,
continuity claims, or energetic semantics.
